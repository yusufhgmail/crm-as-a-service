# CRM From Within brand and logo iterations

**Current decision:** 2026-08-30

## The name changed; the Coremark became more meaningful

Yusuf renamed the CRM business **CRM From Within** and chose `crmfromwithin.com` as the main domain. The earlier Company Native name and its broader software-category thesis now belong to the separate migration business. This CRM brand must be immediately legible to a small company choosing its first CRM while still welcoming customers that need to leave an existing CRM.

Three visual directions were tested against that job:

1. **Evolve the Coremark.** Keep the open evergreen frame, warm coral core, mint, paper and editorial typography. Pair them with the CRM From Within wordmark and explain the mark through the new promise: the useful CRM starts inside the company’s own work.
2. **Draw “within” literally.** Replace the mark with nested boxes, an inward arrow or a C/F/W monogram. This made the name easier to decode at first glance but produced a generic software icon, lost the human warmth of the coral core and became weaker at favicon size.
3. **Use only an editorial wordmark.** Let the name carry the idea without a symbol. This felt warm and direct but removed the recognisable product mark from browser tabs, interfaces and small social placements.

Direction 1 is the strongest. The existing Coremark already contains the new name’s idea more exactly than it expressed the old one: the coral square is the customer’s knowledge and way of working; the open frame is the CRM built around it; the opening shows that the system can keep changing. Preserving it also keeps visual continuity while the name and domains change.

## Current brand decision

- **Company:** CRM From Within
- **Domain:** `crmfromwithin.com`; `crmfromwithin.ai` redirects to it
- **Primary promise:** Your first CRM, built around how you work.
- **Brand idea:** The CRM starts inside the company—not inside a generic template.
- **Primary mark:** The Coremark—an open adaptive frame around a distinct company core
- **Personality:** Observant, practical, warm, calm and owner-minded
- **Primary palette:** Within Ink, Warm Paper, Signal Mint and Core Coral
- **Typography:** Georgia for editorial headlines; Geist Sans for everything operational; Geist Mono for small system labels
- **Brand architecture:** One CRM From Within brand across the current service and the final self-builder vision. Migration is a secondary service into a CRM From Within build, not the lead story.

This visual foundation remains frozen until customer behavior exposes a material problem. The public name, domain and product language are no longer Company Native.

## Historical development of the Coremark

The rounds below record how the symbol and visual system were developed under the previous business name. They explain why the current mark exists; they do not define the current company name or positioning.

## The buyer we designed for

The primary buyer is a founder, CEO, COO, commercial leader or CRM owner in a Swedish or international B2B company. The CRM has become central to sales, delivery or service, but the company is now working around the software. This buyer wants more freedom, but is rightly worried about migration risk, disruption, security, cost and vendor lock-in.

The brand must make the ambition feel large and the first step feel safe. It should signal that Company Native understands the operating reality behind CRM, not only software development.

## Round 1 - the existing identity

**What the buyer sees:** A dark green rounded square with the letters CN. The palette feels mature and the website feels thoughtful.

**How it feels:** Credible enough for a small consultancy, but not distinctive enough for a company trying to define a new category. The initials do not carry the central idea and could belong to almost any business.

**Change:** Keep the strong dark evergreen, warm paper, mint and coral palette. Replace the monogram with a symbol that expresses the actual product truth: software adapting around the company.

## Round 2 - adaptive motion

**What the buyer sees:** Curves and modular shapes moving around a center.

**How it feels:** More dynamic and more connected to adaptation, but several directions read as an eye, an orbit, a network or an AI symbol. Those meanings create the wrong expectations and are already common in software branding.

**Change:** Remove movement for its own sake. Use one stable company core and one precise surrounding structure. Avoid brains, sparkles, networks, puzzle pieces, arrows and circuits.

## Round 3 - the portal

**What the buyer sees:** Layered panels enclosing a central core, like a portal or a book opening.

**How it feels:** Ambitious and product-like, but too many parts weaken the mark at small sizes. It can also suggest a migration gateway rather than an enduring system.

**Change:** Reduce the idea to two elements: a single adaptive frame and a distinct square core. The frame should feel engineered; the core should feel human and specific.

## Round 4 - the Coremark

**What the buyer sees:** An open, structured frame around one warm square.

**How it feels:** The frame creates safety without enclosure. The square says that the company remains the center. The open side suggests that the system can keep changing. The shape is simple enough for a favicon and meaningful enough for a category brand.

**Change:** Use deep evergreen for the system, coral for the company core, mint for the reversed frame, and warm paper as the default field. Pair the mark with a confident Geist Sans wordmark instead of stylized lettering.

## Round 5 - trust and accessibility

**What the buyer sees:** A warm editorial identity rather than a cold enterprise-software brand.

**How it feels:** Credible for an operational change, but still optimistic. Coral calls attention to action and the company core; mint signals progress and system status. The serif headlines add humanity while the sans-serif interface language stays exact.

**Change:** Use coral sparingly. Never put white body text on coral because the contrast is too low. Use Native Ink on coral. Keep diagrams simple and process-led. Use real documentary photography only when it adds evidence.

## Round 6 - cold-visitor and first-10-customer test

**What the buyer sees:** Company Native, the Coremark and the line “Software built around your company.” The website immediately narrows that vision to custom CRM and the safe V0-to-V1 path.

**How it feels:** The company appears to have a larger thesis without making the first offer vague. The visual system can cover the website, social profiles, founder-led outreach, an assessment, a one-pager and a sales conversation without needing a second sub-brand.

**Material improvement check:** Another visual motif would add novelty, not clarity, trust or usefulness. More colors or decorative elements would make the brand less disciplined. A CRM-specific product name would weaken the Company Native category thesis. No material improvement remains.

## Frozen decision

- **Company:** Company Native
- **Category thesis:** Company-native software
- **First offer:** Company-native CRM
- **Tagline:** Software built around your company.
- **Primary mark:** The Coremark - an adaptive frame around a distinct company core
- **Personality:** Ambitious, exact, warm, calm and owner-minded
- **Primary palette:** Native Ink, Warm Paper, Signal Mint and Core Coral
- **Typography:** Georgia for editorial headlines; Geist Sans for everything operational; Geist Mono for small system labels
- **Brand architecture:** One Company Native brand. CRM remains the first offer on the main website.

This foundation is frozen until real customer behavior exposes a material problem. Preference alone is not enough reason to change it.
