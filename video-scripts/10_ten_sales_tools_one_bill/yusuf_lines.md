## Part 1: The usual stack

So you start with a CRM. Then someone wants calls recorded, so you add Gong. And then someone wants automatic follow-up emails, so you add Outreach. Then you need phone numbers and emails for new leads, so you add ZoomInfo. And then comes a dialer, a booking link and e-signatures. Then a proposal tool, a forecasting tool, and a dashboard tool, because none of the others shows the whole picture.

Each one was a good decision on the day you made it. The problem is the arrows. Every arrow is basically data getting copied between tools, and every copy is a place where things go wrong.

Here's a check you can do today. Write down every sales tool you pay for. Next to each one, add the columns on screen. Then ask finance for every software charge from the last year. Most teams find at least one tool nobody opens anymore.

## Part 2: Add up the bill

OK, let's price the big four for ten reps. These are the prices from when I recorded this. They'll change, so they're just an example. HubSpot Sales Hub Professional was a hundred dollars a seat per month. So for ten reps, that's twelve thousand a year. Plus fifteen hundred to get started.

Now, Gong, Outreach and ZoomInfo don't show prices at all. You just fill in a form and someone calls you. What I'm giving you next are dated public estimates, mostly from competitors. So treat it as a ballpark, not a quote.

Gong starts from about twenty thousand a year for ten reps. In year one, with setup, it's often thirty thousand or more. Vendr, which sees real contracts, puts the median at about fifty-five thousand a year. Outreach is a hundred to a hundred and sixty dollars per user per month. For ten reps, that's twelve to about nineteen thousand a year. And ZoomInfo's entry plan is about twenty-two and a half thousand for five users. That's from a quote someone shared in March.

So add those four up, and you get around sixty-six to eighty-four thousand dollars a year, with the top end in year one. And that's before the other six boxes. For a lot of companies, that's a full salary.

Now go back to your own list, and write one total for the year. Then circle the first renewal, because that's the one you can change.

## Part 3: The hidden cost

The bigger cost isn't on the invoices. It's the time. Somebody actually has to keep ten tools connected. One of them changes a field, and a sync breaks. Then the dashboard doesn't match the CRM, and a meeting turns into an argument about whose number is right.

Someone who runs sales at a large software company told me about his setup. A consultant had hard-coded his CRM. Every change was done by hand. And the data didn't add up. So his idea was one product, with the calls, the emails and the contact data inside the CRM. Not ten subscriptions that each think they own the customer.

That's the hidden cost. With ten tools, you've got ten copies of your customer, and none of them is the full picture.

## Part 4: One system
