## LinkedIn text posts

### Post 1 · story
A company upgraded to Salesforce Enterprise.

They paid over ten thousand dollars for a custom portal for their sales reps. It never did what they needed, and was abandoned.

Then Salesforce wouldn't let them go back to a cheaper plan.

And while they were moving to HubSpot, in their words, they learned Salesforce had deleted all their data.

That's one story from the study we just did. We read public posts and reviews from more than five thousand CRM buyers. HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho, Pipedrive, and dozens more. We kept only people who use or pay for a CRM, and we counted each person once.

Almost three thousand complained about price. You could say everyone complains about price, and fair enough. But only around six hundred complained about price and nothing else. More than eleven hundred tied it to a renewal, a tier jump or seat costs. So often it's not "this is expensive". It's "it keeps going up, and we can't do anything about it."

Take three years of CRM invoices. For every increase, write down why. Because you grew? Or because they changed the price, the tier or the terms?

The second part is the one you don't control.

Which of your increases were because you grew, and which because they changed the terms?

### Post 2 · how-to
I've set up CRMs for more than 450 companies. Here are three checks you can do this week, whatever CRM you use.

1. Limits. Find your plan's limits: contacts, leads, users, records, automations. Write today's usage next to each. Anything above seventy percent is a tier jump waiting to happen. More than six hundred buyers hit a limit and found the only way past it was a much pricier plan.

2. Clicks. Take your team's three most common tasks. Log a call, create a quote, move a deal. Count the clicks. More than twelve hundred buyers said their CRM is too hard to use.

3. Your process. Draw how a customer moves through your business, from first call to paid invoice. Under each step, write where it really lives: in the CRM, in a spreadsheet, or in someone's head.

Every box that isn't "CRM" is a workaround your team has just accepted.

Nearly nineteen hundred buyers in our study said their CRM just doesn't fit how they work. This third check shows you if you're one of them.

Which of the three would you expect to find something in first?

### Post 3 · opinion
Here's the surprise from our study of more than five thousand CRM buyers: many of the people who complain never leave.

About six in ten are still on their CRM. One says: "I'm a bit stuck since I've started using zoho already and don't have the time to switch."

They're not leaving, they're stuck. Switching feels like a project nobody has time for.

Switching can be the right call for some. But of the buyers who posted about a switch, more than five hundred regret it. One company says switching to Salesforce was the worst decision they ever made.

Another CRM comes with the same seats, tiers and renewals.

So before you switch, figure out which of the ten reasons is really your problem. Then sort it. Is it a setting you can fix today? A tier you negotiate at renewal? Or a fit problem, where no plan fixes it?

Fit was number two in our ranking. Nearly nineteen hundred buyers said their CRM just doesn't fit how they work.

Which of the ten sounds most like your company?
