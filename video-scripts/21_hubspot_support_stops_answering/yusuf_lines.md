## Step 1: Know what your plan promises

Number one, find out what your HubSpot plan actually promises you. Their terms only say they'll try to reply within one business day, and that's not a promise. So after a couple of days, start chasing them.

In a CRM you own, your team may not even need to ask support, because the AI in it already knows your process.

## Step 2: Go to the right team

Number two, take your problem to the team that can actually fix it. When support goes quiet, most people just keep writing to support, but a lot of these aren't support problems at all. So ask, is something broken, or is it about money? Broken things go to support. A bill goes to billing, and your renewal goes to the team that handles contracts.

But what if they don't answer either? Here's another real example from Trustpilot, in May. One company emailed HubSpot for almost a month and got nowhere, until they called the sales line. Salespeople pick up the phone, because that's how they make money. So if it's been quiet for a week, or you're locked out, go to sales.

But don't ask them to fix it, because they usually can't. Just ask who at HubSpot is looking after this, so you have one name to keep going back to.

On your own CRM, you'd just talk to the team that built it, and with AI, they can usually change it the same day.

## Step 3: Put it in writing

Number three, put your problem in writing, because if you end up arguing about a bill, you'll need to show what you said. And if you're disputing a bill, say so, because HubSpot's terms say they won't suspend you while you're genuinely disputing it.

## Step 4: Stop the renewal

Number four, have your Super Admin turn off auto-renewal, because otherwise you could pay for up to another year of this. That doesn't mean you're leaving, because you can turn it back on before your plan ends.

And think about what you'd be renewing. Say your CRM has a hundred features and you use three. You're paying for ninety-seven features that you're never going to use. When you own your CRM, you only build what you need, so you get a way better system at a way lower cost.

## Step 5: Get your data out now

Number five, get your data out now, while your account still works, because paying up front didn't protect that first company. I know this is kind of the boring part. But you just want a copy, so your team could keep selling if they got locked out.

So ask one of your salespeople, if HubSpot was gone on Monday, what would you need? It's usually your customers, your open deals and who to call next. So export those with the option on screen, because otherwise you don't get everything.

If you owned your own CRM, nobody could suspend you, and your data would already be yours. And with AI, you can literally have a system that updates itself, so your salespeople spend their time on customers, not on admin.

## Building your CRM on the side

So those five steps get you through this problem, but your next ticket goes into the same queue. You might think a slow ticket is no reason to build a CRM, but think about the last time your team sat waiting. Which deals went quiet?

Here's how building on the side works, with a made-up example. Say new leads aren't reaching the right salesperson, and support isn't answering. So you build one small lead tool. New leads land there first, it gives each one to the right salesperson, and it passes them on to HubSpot. So your team works the way they always do, but every new lead is now in a system you own. And when you want that rule changed, you just tell the people who built it.

Then you build the next piece. And one day you simply notice that, wow, all our data is actually in this new system that we own. So shutting off HubSpot is basically zero risk.
