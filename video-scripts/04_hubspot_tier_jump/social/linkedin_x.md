### Post 2 · how-to
Before you upgrade HubSpot, write down one number: your year-one jump.

An upgrade can be the right call. The mistake is making it without that number.

First, write the job. Not "we need Enterprise". The job. Something like, our sales manager approves any discount over ten percent, about twenty times a month.

Then work out the cost: new seats at the new price, plus every other Core seat at its new price, times twelve, plus onboarding.

Remember, HubSpot's own catalog says Core seats are priced at the highest tier you have, across every hub. Upgrade one hub to Professional and every Core seat goes from twenty dollars to fifty.

Next, email your HubSpot rep four questions, and keep the replies:
1. How long is the commitment, and when does it start?
2. Does this change or extend our current renewal date?
3. What is the onboarding fee?
4. What can we remove at renewal?

Finally, next to your jump number, write three smaller ways to get the job done, and what each costs. A smaller version on your tier. The feature for only the people who need it. Or one tool for one job. Very often one of them is a fraction of the jump.

What's the one feature that pushed you, or almost pushed you, up a tier?

### Post 3 · opinion
Skipping one HubSpot upgrade is not the end of it.

Higher tiers do give you real features, and sometimes the upgrade is right. But it's a staircase. You save yourself from one jump. Next year there's another feature, and another step.

Starter seats are twenty dollars a month. Professional is up to a hundred. Enterprise is a hundred and fifty. That's five times the price per seat, and on Professional you go from a plan you can leave next month to a contract for a year.

And if what you need isn't on any tier at all, going up a step doesn't fix anything.

In our study, more than three hundred HubSpot customers complained about the jump from one tier to the next. Nearly four hundred complained that it just doesn't fit how they work. That's more.

### Post 6 · how-to
Before you take the whole step up a HubSpot tier, look for a smaller one, in three places.

One. Is there a smaller version on your tier? Sales Hub Starter today has up to fifty simpler workflows, and up to fifteen pipelines. A pipeline is the list of stages a deal moves through, say one for new customers and one for renewals. If your job is simple, that might be enough.

Two. Buy it only for the people who actually use it. Only the sales seats get the full sales features. Everyone else can sit on a cheaper Core seat. But test it first. One company was told to keep one Professional seat and put everyone else on cheaper seats. Then only that one person could make calls.

Three. An app from HubSpot's marketplace, or a tool you already pay for that connects to HubSpot. Then you pay for one job, and not a whole tier.

Next to your jump number, write three smaller ways to get the job done, and what each costs. Very often one of them is a fraction of the jump.

Test the exact job, with the exact people, before you sign.
