### Post 2 · how-to
The thirty-minute map. Five steps, with the people who do the work.

1. Pick one real job (5 min). One customer, already paid.
2. Write every step (10 min). One sticky note per step, each starting with a verb, from first contact to the money in the bank.
3. Mark where each step lives (5 min). Green sticker if it happens in the CRM. Red if it's anywhere else: a spreadsheet, someone's inbox, a text message, the whiteboard, paper, or someone's head.
4. Draw the re-typing (5 min). Every time the same information gets typed again, draw a red arrow. Put a number on it: times a week, times minutes.
5. Sort every red one (5 min): setting, tier or fit.

On our demo roofing company, the map had eleven steps. The CRM board ended at Closed Won, step six of eleven. The first call and the signed contract were green. Two green, nine red.

Around two hundred buyers in our study wrote about the spreadsheets and the re-typing they do next to their CRM.

Ask the person who does the re-typing. They know the number exactly.

Which step on your map would turn out red first?

### Post 3 · opinion
Every red sticky note on your map is one of three things: a setting, a tier, or a fit problem.

A setting is something your CRM can already do, and nobody set it up. Fix it this week.

A tier is something your CRM can do, on a bigger plan. You decide it at renewal, with the numbers. One company needed one more pipeline, and said the next level up cost around five thousand dollars more a year.

A fit problem is one where no setting and no plan does it your way. Your insurance approvals. Your crew schedule. Your estimate turning into your invoice with your rules.

One CEO spent over sixteen thousand dollars on HubSpot and worked with HubSpot's own team for more than a year. Their answer: "We had to ask if the tool fit our workflow. For us, it just didn't."

In our study, nearly nineteen hundred CRM buyers said their CRM doesn't fit how they work.

The S's are this week's to-do list. The T's go on your renewal list. And the F's, with the hours next to them, are what your CRM really doesn't do for you.

Which letter would you find the most of on your own map?
