Title: Video script rules

[All video scripts](README.md)

Yusuf's corrections to the scripts. Read this before writing or changing a script, and follow it in every new video, whoever writes it.

# Spoken lines

**Announce every example.** A listener can't see what "this company" points at. Say it's an example or that we found it in our research, then say "one company", not "this company".

- Not like this: "This company missed a renewal quote."
- But like this: "Here's an example from our research. One company missed a renewal quote."

**Open with the problem.** "Who it's for" names a problem viewers recognize, using what the research found where we can, then says what the video does about it.

- Not like this: "If you're on HubSpot, or you're about to sign, and you've never seen the whole bill in one place, this one's for you."
- But like this: "In that study, a lot of people said they're confused about HubSpot's pricing. So this video clears that up: what a twenty-person team really pays, so nothing surprises you."

**Keep the hook, change the words.** We're making 20 videos, so viewers would hear a repeated sentence again and again. Every video still needs a reason to watch to the end. Promise something specific from the end of that video: a question it answers, a callback to the opening story, or a surprise you hold back. Check that the video delivers it, and search the other scripts so the wording and the shape are new.

- Not like this: "And stay for step five."
- But like this: "And the last one isn't a cost at all. It's the test that tells you if forty thousand dollars a year is a great deal or a terrible one."

**Every line earns its place.** People want information, not filler. Each line must give the viewer something or do a job for us. Keep the fit check and the comment question to one short sentence, and word them differently in each video.

- Not like this: "So here's your homework." or "If you watch this and change nothing, nothing changes."
- But like this: "Your move: book thirty minutes with your team this week."

**Pitch in this order.** Many viewers don't know who we are, so say what we do before saying who doesn't need us. Tie it to this video's problem, say who we are and what they get, say what makes it easy and safe (we move the data, they own the code, no long contract), say who it's for, then give one next step: the fit check.

- Not like this: "If you're a two-person team, you don't need us." before saying what we do.
- But like this: "That's why I started CRM From Within. We build you a CRM around how your company sells and delivers. We move your data over and we maintain it. You own the code, there's no long contract, and if you leave, you take everything. It's for companies of ten to fifty people that a standard CRM doesn't fit. If a standard setup works for you, keep it."

**Keep it true later.** Videos stay online for years, and prices and plans change. Give the total instead of the pricing model when you can. Say once when the prices were checked, call them an example, and put a "Prices as of" tag on price screens. Never say "today", "this month" or "this October".

- Not like this: "This is HubSpot's own page today. Ninety to a hundred dollars a seat per month."
- But like this: "Here's HubSpot's own page when I recorded this, in November 2026. For twenty people, that's about eighteen hundred to two thousand dollars a month. Prices change, so treat that as an example."
