## 0:00 Hook

**ON SCREEN:** Yusuf on camera. Tag: **Replace your CRM in one big project?**

**YUSUF** (straight to camera):
> Many of you have been talking to us about replacing your entire CRM in one big project. I'd strongly recommend against that. Here's how I'd do it instead, and why.

## 0:11 Why you were stuck

**ON SCREEN:** The old CRM box, whole. A big arrow to an empty box: **One big move** with tags **All data at once** · **Everyone retrained** · **If it fails, nobody can sell**

**YUSUF:**
> Until recently, one big project was pretty much the only way to leave a CRM. You moved all your data and you retrained everyone in one go, and if that went wrong, your salespeople couldn't sell. That's a huge risk. So you really had no choice, and you stayed in the old CRM, even when you could see it was costing you deals.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **Example: a Swedish solar startup** (no name). Then, one at a time: **~15 salespeople** · **About $30,000 a year for HubSpot** · **Used: the pipeline** · **Leads reached late or not at all**

**YUSUF:**
> A friend of mine works at a solar startup in Sweden. They had about fifteen salespeople, and they were paying about thirty thousand dollars a year for HubSpot. All they used was the pipeline, a board where salespeople picked up leads by hand. A lot of leads came in, but the team got to them late or not at all. So they lost a lot of business, and management had no clue.

**ON SCREEN:** tags, one at a time: **Renewal in 2 months** · **New system must work by then** · **If it fails, the business stops**

**YUSUF:**
> They wanted to replace HubSpot with their own system, and I told them we could build it. But their HubSpot renewal was two months away, so the new system had to work by then. And all their leads went through that pipeline, so if it failed, their whole business would stop. That was too big a risk, so they couldn't even start.

## 1:27 What changed

**ON SCREEN:** tag only: **Hundreds of CRM setups · Built & sold a CRM SaaS**. Then: **Now: builds custom CRMs for you**. Then: **Pay for 100 features, use 3**

**YUSUF:**
> I built a CRM SaaS company, and I sold it in 2025. Me and my team set up CRMs for hundreds of companies. Now, I run a company that builds custom CRMs for you. So I know how hard it is to move a whole sales team onto something new.
>
> But AI has changed what it costs to build a CRM. A CRM that's sold to everybody needs a lot of features, let's say a hundred, and every customer only uses about three. So you pay for the hundred. With AI, I realized it's now cheaper to build just the three you need, and that's why I sold my old company.

## 2:07 How you leave a CRM now

**ON SCREEN:** The old CRM box with an empty new box beside it, a line between them. One piece moves across, then another: **Your team keeps working in the old CRM** · **One piece at a time** · **Doesn't work? Switch it off**

**YUSUF:**
> So you're not stuck any more, because you don't have to move everything at once. At my company, CRM From Within, we build your new CRM from scratch, step by step, while your team keeps working in the old CRM on the stuff they're used to. We build one piece, your team starts using it, and the data for that piece moves over. If a piece doesn't work, you switch it off, and your team goes back to what they did before.

**ON SCREEN:** the old CRM box, empty. The new box, full: **All your data** · **You own it** · **Full control**. Then a switch on the old box: **Basically zero risk**

**YUSUF:**
> We keep going like that, until one day you notice that all your data is already in the new system. That's a system you own, and you have full control over it. So at that point, it's basically zero risk to just shut off your old CRM.
>
> I understand why you'd still want to switch everything in one go, because then you're done with the old CRM and its bill. And yes, going step by step means you pay for both for a while. I think that's worth it, because you see the new system working before your business depends on it.

## 3:18 Where to start

**ON SCREEN:** section title: **Where to start**. Then: **Biggest opportunity, least cost**. Then a card: **Lost at each step → deals that would have closed**. Tag: **Example: 20 lost leads, 1 in 5 closes = 4 lost deals**. Then: **DO THIS TODAY: most lost deals, cheapest fix = first step**

**YUSUF:**
> We start by going through what your company needs, and then we take the biggest opportunity for the least cost first. You can do this part yourself. Go through last month's leads and deals, and work out how many deals you lost at each step, like the first call, the demo and the quote. Include the leads nobody called.
>
> Most leads never close anyway, so don't just count leads. Say you lost twenty leads at one step, and one in five normally closes. That's four lost deals. The step where you lost the most deals goes first. And if two steps are close, start with the one that's cheaper to build.

**ON SCREEN:** the old CRM box. A small new box appears beside it: **First call**. Tag: **Every minute of waiting makes the deal less likely**. Then, a simple flow: **AI voice talks to the lead → asks a few questions → connects the lead to a free salesperson**. Then: **Renew HubSpot, add the AI voice**

**YUSUF:**
> At that solar startup, the step was the first call, because the longer a lead waits, the less likely you are to win the deal. So we talked about an AI voice that talks to every lead straight away. It asks a few questions, and it connects the lead to a salesperson the moment one's free.
>
> That only replaces the first call, so they could've renewed HubSpot and just added the AI voice. And I think that would've really helped their sales.

## 4:32 Make a roadmap

**ON SCREEN:** section title: **Make a roadmap**. A row of boxes in order, the first ticked: **What comes first · What comes next**. Tag: **Your CRM, not another company's features**

**YUSUF:**
> Then we put the rest of your steps in order in a roadmap. Over time you own your CRM, and you stop depending on another company's features. If you want something changed, you just change it.

## 4:46 What can go wrong

**ON SCREEN:** section title: **What can go wrong**. The line between the two boxes, with a red break in it. Red tag: **The connection breaks**. Tag: **Built differently: everything gets translated**. Tag: **Example: own quoting software** (no name). Then: **Connection fails → no quotes go out**

**YUSUF:**
> What usually goes wrong while you're running two systems is the connection between them. Setting up CRMs for hundreds of companies taught me that connections are what break most, on any CRM. Connecting two systems sounds like a small job, but they're built differently, so everything has to be translated from one to the other. For example, one company we worked with had built its own quoting software next to the CRM. When that connection failed, they just couldn't send quotes.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **Count the systems**. Then: **1 or 2 systems? You can probably do it · More? Connect fewer**. Then: **Who fixes it when it breaks?**

**YUSUF:**
> The more systems a step connects to, the more can break. So count them. If it's one or two, you can probably do it. If it's more, try to connect that step to fewer systems.
>
> And before you start, decide who fixes it when it breaks. If your developer leaves or a freelancer only signed up for the build, year two can be a disaster. And if you're the one who'd fix it, and you're also running your company, it's probably not the best idea.

## 5:50 When to shut off the old CRM

**ON SCREEN:** section title: **When to shut off the old CRM**. The two boxes, one step ticked. Then: **DO THIS AFTER 60 DAYS: fewer lost deals? No fixes by hand?**

**YUSUF:**
> Once your first step is running, you'll probably want to do the next one straight away. I'd give it sixty days first, so you've seen enough deals to know if it's really working. Then count last month's lost deals at that step again. If there are clearly fewer than before, and nobody's had to fix the connection by hand, do the next step on your roadmap. If not, fix what's wrong first.

**ON SCREEN:** the old CRM box, empty. Tag: **Nobody works in it? Shut it off**. Then tag: **Example from our research: a mortgage business**. Then, one at a time: **Built their own small CRM around how they sell** · **Still on HubSpot: marketing only** · **HubSpot contract down 75%**

**YUSUF:**
> Once nobody's working in the old CRM any more, you can shut it off.
>
> One mortgage business in our research has already moved its sales off HubSpot. With a small team of developers and AI, they built their own small CRM around how they sell. Now they only use HubSpot for marketing, and their contract came down seventy-five percent.
