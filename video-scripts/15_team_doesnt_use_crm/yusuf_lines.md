# 3 Reasons Your Team Doesn't Use the CRM, and the Fix

*Video 15 · Yusuf's lines only*

## Proof

At my old company, our notes on one customer said their sales team used the CRM. Two years later they cancelled, and our notes said they weren't using it at all. By then nobody was adding deals, and some of their deals were in Excel.

I built a CRM SaaS company, and I sold it in 2025. Me and my team set up CRMs for hundreds of companies, and we still didn't see that this team had stopped using it.

## Who it's for

So your team could be working around your CRM right now, and you might not know. And that can cost you deals. For example, one business owner on a German Reddit forum brought in HubSpot. A year later, the salespeople were entering nothing. Then a deal fell apart, because nobody knew the customer had been waiting three weeks for a quote. The salespeople said, I've got it all in my head. Or, that just costs time.

The owner asked if they'd hired the wrong people. I don't think so. The CRM costs the salespeople time and gives them very little back, so pushing them harder won't fix it.

## Plan

There are three reasons a team stops using its CRM, and you can start fixing each one this week. I'll show you in HubSpot, but it works in any CRM. Then I'll show you how to find out if your CRM can be fixed at all.

## Reason 1: Too many fields

The first reason is that salespeople have too many fields to fill in. During setup, each manager adds the fields they want for their report, and nobody asks the salesperson what they need to close a deal.

A founder posted on X that their Salesforce has a hundred and twenty-seven custom fields. Their top seller uses three, and her deals close. The founder says the rest are there so the operations team can build a dashboard no one opens.

And all those fields take time. One sales rep on Reddit says he spends thirty to forty minutes a day on CRM admin, and it does nothing for him. Even at thirty minutes, that's about a hundred and twenty-five hours a year, for one rep. Small companies simply don't have time to sit and log data.

So the question I'd ask is, what can we cut?

Start with the required fields, because a rep can't save a deal until those are filled in. Write down every required field, and next to each one, write who reads it and what they do with it.

Then ask your best salesperson which of these they actually use to close a deal. If they don't use a field and nobody else does anything with it, make it optional.

In HubSpot's pipeline settings, you just untick Required.

## Reason 2: It doesn't match how they work

The second reason is that the stages in your CRM aren't the steps your team really does. So they just do the job and skip the CRM. The mistake I see most is letting the CRM control your process. Start with how you sell, and adapt the CRM to that.

Say you're a roofer. One of HubSpot's default stages is Presentation Scheduled. But a roofer doesn't do presentations. They book inspections, write estimates and wait for the insurance approval. So they can't tell which stage a deal belongs in, and they stop moving deals.

So ask your team what they call each step, and change the stages to match, in the same pipeline settings.

## Reason 3: Everything gets typed twice

The third reason is that your team has to type everything twice. They work in their inbox and on the phone, and then they have to type every email and call into the CRM again.

One sales manager on Reddit says every phone call meant more fields to update, so after a few weeks, people went back to Outlook reminders and their own notes.

So make sure they only have to type things once. First, each rep connects their inbox, which works on any HubSpot plan. Then they can send and log emails from the CRM, instead of copying them over.

A rep still has to log calls, but they can tick Create a To-do and pick the date. Now they've got a list of who to call back and when, so they don't need those Outlook reminders.

## Find out if your CRM can be fixed

Now that the CRM is less work, you can push. Tell your team, "From Monday, we run the sales meeting from the CRM. If a deal isn't in it, we don't talk about it."

Then give it two weeks. If deals still aren't going in, sit down with each person, one at a time, and ask, "What would make the CRM save you time?" And write down exactly what they say.

If you can do what they ask in the settings, do it this week. If it needs a higher plan, compare the extra cost with what one lost deal costs you. But if the CRM can't do it on any plan, the CRM doesn't fit, and your team is right to avoid it.

## When the CRM can't do the job

You can sometimes force a CRM to fit, but it takes a lot of work. I did that for a recruitment firm. They find salespeople for other companies, so they deal with the companies hiring and the people looking for jobs. A standard setup couldn't show which person was up for which job, so their team stopped using it.

I got them using it again, but I had to sit with them until I understood their process, build one list for the companies and one for the people, link them with automations, and then train their team one stage at a time.

I think the real problem with adapting a standard system is that you're always working inside its limits.

## Built around the work

If you build a CRM around the job instead, your team does the work inside it. They write the quote in the CRM, so there's nothing to go back and log. And anyone can see which customers are still waiting for a quote, so nobody waits three weeks without you knowing.

One staffing firm, Coconut, did this. They spent over sixteen thousand dollars on HubSpot, and after more than a year of working with HubSpot's own team, they say it just didn't fit how they work. So they built their own CRM in forty-five days. Everyone sent in a wishlist, their developer built from that, and they say the team loves it.

Coconut had its own developer. If you don't, you can still build from the ground up, with a company that's done it many times. It's much easier to get exactly what you want without all the bloat.
