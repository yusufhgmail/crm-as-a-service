## 1:25 Reason 1: Too much work, nothing back

**ON SCREEN:** reason tracker: **Reason 1 of 3: Too much work, nothing back**. Tag: **Fields for the report, not for the rep**

**YUSUF:**
> Reason one, and it's the biggest one. Too many fields and too many clicks for the person who has to fill them in. Because during setup, every manager adds a bunch of fields they'd like for their report. But nobody asks the salesperson what they need to close the deal.

**ON SCREEN:** the give/take scale: **Puts in: 12 fields per call** (tag **example**) · **Gets back: nothing**

**YUSUF:**
> Small companies simply don't have time to sit and log data. Every field costs the rep a little bit of time. And if they get nothing back, they stop. That isn't laziness, it's just maths, right? The rep pays every single day. The only one who gets anything back is the manager on Monday morning. So once the spreadsheet is less work, the spreadsheet wins.

**ON SCREEN:** whiteboard table: **Field · Who reads it · What they do with it**, three example rows, the last one **"nobody"** in red. Tag: **Not deleted. Just not required.**

**YUSUF:**
> The fix is a field audit. List every field your team must fill in. Next to each one, write who reads it and what they do with it. If the answer is nobody, it stops being required. You don't delete it, you just make it optional.

**ON SCREEN:** screen recording (upbridge portal), zoomed: Settings → Data Management → Objects → Deals → **Pipelines** tab → **Sales Pipeline**. Arrow tag: **Conditional stage properties** → **Edit properties**. Red box on **Required**; cursor unticks it. Tag: **Required = can't save without it**. **DO THIS: list every required field + who reads it**
Link: https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/set-up-and-customize-pipelines

**YUSUF:**
> In HubSpot, go to your pipeline settings. The path is on screen. On each stage, you'll see the fields a deal needs before it can move on. Required means they can't save the deal without it. Every HubSpot plan has this. So untick every field that failed the audit.

**ON SCREEN:** card: **SAY THIS (to your best salesperson):** "Which three fields do you actually use to close a deal? Everything else is going optional."

**YUSUF:**
> Then ask your best salesperson, "Which three fields do you actually use to close a deal? Everything else is going optional." And whatever they say, keep those three required.

## 3:45 Reason 2: It doesn't match how they work

**ON SCREEN:** reason tracker: **Reason 2 of 3: It doesn't match the work**. Tag: **Stages ≠ steps**

**YUSUF:**
> Reason two. The CRM doesn't match how the team works. The stages aren't the steps of the job, so they just do the job and skip the CRM. And usually that's because someone who doesn't sell every day set them up from a template.

**ON SCREEN:** split screen. Left: the demo roofer's step map from video 6 (tag **From video 6**). Right: upbridge Deals board, seven default stages; red box on **Presentation Scheduled** and **Decision Maker Bought-In**; tag **Not how a roofer works**

**YUSUF:**
> So here are the default stages in HubSpot for our demo roofer, which is the made-up company from video six. Look at this one, "Presentation scheduled." A roofer doesn't do presentations. They book an inspection, they write an estimate, and then they wait for the insurance approval. So this is where your sticky-note map from that video comes in.

**ON SCREEN:** screen recording (upbridge portal), zoomed: Settings → Data Management → Objects → Deals → **Pipelines** tab → **Sales Pipeline**. Arrow tag on a stage name; he renames **Presentation Scheduled** to **Inspection booked** and **Decision Maker Bought-In** to **Estimate sent**. Tag: **Stage = a step your team really does**. **DO THIS: rename every stage to a real step + 2 yes/no questions per stage**
Link: https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/set-up-and-customize-pipelines

**YUSUF:**
> Here's the fix. Rename every stage to a step your team really does, in their own words. Then give each stage two yes-or-no questions. For example, "Inspection photos taken?" And "Estimate sent to the adjuster?" You check those in your weekly meeting. And you don't have to do it all at once. Start with the stage where deals pile up.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **When renaming isn't enough**

**YUSUF:**
> But sometimes you rename every stage and it still doesn't fit, because the job itself isn't in the CRM. And in that case, your team has a point.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **Example: a recruitment firm for salespeople** (no name). Whiteboard, two pipelines drawn as he says them: **Clients: lead → needs interview → hiring project** · **Recruits: found → prequalified → presented → interviewed → hired**, with a line linking each recruit to a client's project.

**YUSUF:**
> Let me give you an example. I worked with a recruitment firm that finds salespeople for other companies. Their team had stopped using the CRM. They deal with two groups, the companies hiring and the recruits. And each recruit goes through their own steps for every hiring project. A standard setup couldn't link a recruit to a client's project. So the CRM was useless to them.
>
> So I sat with them and learned how their process really works. I proposed two pipelines, one for clients and one for recruits, with some custom fields. Leadership said yes, and I built the automations. Then I trained the sellers, one stage at a time. That's what got them back in.
>
> So if renaming isn't enough, write down what's missing, and we'll come back to it.

## 6:55 Reason 3: It's not where they work

**ON SCREEN:** reason tracker: **Reason 3 of 3: It's not where they work**. Tag: **One more place to type it**

**YUSUF:**
> Reason three. The CRM kind of sits off to the side. Your team lives in their inbox and on the phone. So the CRM is just one more place to type it all again. Every email a rep copies across by hand is another reason to skip it.

**ON SCREEN:** screen recording (upbridge portal), zoomed: Settings → General → **Email** → arrow tag on **Connect email account**. Tag: **All HubSpot plans, free included**. Then a deal record → **Log call** → arrow tag on **Create a To-do** → **Log activity** → CRM → **Tasks**, red box on the new to-do. Tag: **The CRM reminds them**. **DO THIS: connect every inbox + a to-do on every call for a week**
Links: https://knowledge.hubspot.com/connected-email/connect-your-inbox-to-hubspot · https://knowledge.hubspot.com/tasks/use-tasks

**YUSUF:**
> So the first fix is to connect every rep's inbox. It's on every HubSpot plan. Now emails get logged instead of copied over.
>
> The second fix is to give them something back. Each time a rep logs a call, they also create a to-do. Now the CRM tells them who to call back and when. And for the first time, it's actually working for them.

**ON SCREEN:** the give/take scale, now level: **Puts in: fewer fields, inbox connected** · **Gets back: follow-ups, reminders**

**YUSUF:**
> When the teams I've worked with started using the CRM again, it was nearly always in that order. First it saved them time, then the manager started asking for things. So you give first, and then you ask.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **No board, no discussion**

**YUSUF:**
> After that, run your weekly sales meeting from the CRM screen, not from memory. You just open the board and go deal by deal.

**ON SCREEN:** card: **SAY THIS (to the team, before Monday's meeting):** "From Monday, we run this meeting from the board. If a deal isn't on it, we don't talk about it."

**YUSUF:**
> But tell the team first. "From Monday, we run this meeting from the board. If a deal isn't on it, we don't talk about it." Give it two weeks, and the board fills up.

## 10:10 Is fixing it enough?

**ON SCREEN:** two questions written large, the first crossed out: ~~**What can we add?**~~ → **What can we cut?** Then three boxes: **Setting** (fields, stages, inbox) · **Tier** (automatic follow-ups: HubSpot Professional) · **Fit** (the job isn't in the CRM), the last one in red.

**YUSUF:**
> OK, so here's the mistake I promised you. The one I see most is letting the CRM control your process. You should start with how you sell, and then adapt the CRM to support that. You're not supposed to adapt your process to the CRM.
>
> And then people overcomplicate it. They add things because they sound cool. So the main question shouldn't be "What can we add?" It should be "What can we cut?" You want the CRM to support your process with as little admin as possible.
>
> Fields, stages and inboxes are settings, so you can fix those this week. Automatic follow-ups are a tier thing. In HubSpot, that's Professional. But if the CRM can't do the job itself, that's a fit thing. And pressure doesn't fix fit. You just end up paying for seats for people who work somewhere else.
