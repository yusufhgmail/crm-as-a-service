So here are the five steps.

## Step 1: Find your dates

Step one is to find your dates. Your contracts page shows when your contract ends, and the path's on screen. Now, Salesforce's own pages don't agree on your deadline. When I recorded this, the contract said thirty days before the end, and the help page said ninety. I don't really see how those fit together, so I'd go earlier than both. Use a hundred days, and put that in your calendar.

In a CRM you own, there's simply no end date, and nobody can shut it off because you missed a date.

## Step 2: Read your order form

Step two is to read your order form, which is the document you actually signed. If it disagrees with Salesforce's main contract, the order form wins. So your real deadline is whatever the order form says.

## Step 3: Count your licences

Step three is to count your licences, because you can add them any day, but you can only remove them at the renewal. So they just pile up.

But be careful, because if you renew with fewer, Salesforce can set a new price per licence. One customer on Reddit says they halved their licences, and everything went to full list price. So ask Salesforce for the yearly total both ways, and take the lower one.

In a CRM you own, nobody sets a new price when your team gets smaller.

## Step 4: Turn off auto-renew, and get another price

Step four is to turn off auto-renew in writing, a hundred days before the end. I know that can feel like picking a fight. But it doesn't actually cancel Salesforce. It just means you have to sign a new quote before the end date.

When you're selling, a contract that renews by itself is the easiest deal you'll ever get. So when you turn that off, Salesforce suddenly has to give you a reason to sign.

The email to send is on screen and in the description. Then check that it actually worked, because that customer who gave eighty-nine days' notice emailed several times, and their account page still said auto-renew. They say what solved it was going to Salesforce's legal and ethics team. So if your rep won't confirm it's off, I'd go there too.

The same week, get a price from another CRM, because with only one price, you've got nothing to push back with. One company on Reddit showed Salesforce an offer from HubSpot, and they say it was ridiculous how much the price came down.

But if you move to HubSpot, you're still renting, so I'd also get a price for a CRM you own.

## Step 5: Ask if Salesforce fits

Step five is the one I care about most, because a lower price doesn't change how Salesforce fits your work. What you're actually buying is a standard process that you have to adapt to, and nobody's really happy with someone else's process. Out of every hundred companies I worked with, maybe three were happy with the standard setup.

So take one real customer from last month, and write down every step, from the first contact to getting paid. Then mark every step that happened outside Salesforce. Like, was the quote in a spreadsheet? Was the follow-up in someone's head?

You might think, it's just one spreadsheet, no big deal. But that's work you're paying Salesforce for and still doing by hand.

If it's just one or two steps, a lower price may be all you need for now. If it's more than that, a lower price won't fix it, and that's when I'd want my own CRM.
