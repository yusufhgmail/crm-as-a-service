## 3:10 Point 3: What's hype

**ON SCREEN:** point tracker **Point 3 of 5: Hype**. The board, left column, three lines appearing: **"Nobody will pay for software"** · **"AI builds a CRM in an afternoon"** · **"Vendors are giving up seats"**

**YUSUF:**
> So let's look at the hype. Number one is "nobody will pay for software anymore." That's just wrong. Companies will keep paying for software that does a job. The real question is what they pay per head.
>
> Number two, "AI builds your CRM in an afternoon." And sure, it can build a working screen in an afternoon. But a CRM your business runs on for five years needs a lot more. Your data has to move properly. You need permissions and backups. And you need someone to fix it when it breaks.

**ON SCREEN:** Salesforce Agentforce pricing page, zoomed: **$2 per conversation · $500 per 100k Credits · Agentforce add-ons $125 user/month**. Tag: **Prices as of Nov 2026**
Link: https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/

**YUSUF:**
> Number three is "the vendors are dropping seats." But just look at Salesforce's own AI pricing page. They charge two dollars per conversation, and there's still a price per user as well. HubSpot's plans now come with credits too. So the seat isn't gone, they're just stacking usage charges on top of it.

**ON SCREEN:** Yusuf's blog post, zoomed on the date **June 2023**, then **"costs will not disappear"**, then **"The dominant players will still be price setters"**. Tag: **My post · June 2023**. Then: **DON'T WAIT for your bill to fall**
Link: https://yusufyoung.com/how-the-software-industry-will-change-when-development-cost-nears-zero-who-will-still-make-money/

**YUSUF:**
> And I actually wrote about this in June 2023, almost three years before that selloff. The post is linked below. My point was that when building software gets close to free, the costs don't disappear, they just move. Finding customers and running at scale stay expensive. So the big vendors keep setting the price. That means you shouldn't wait for your CRM bill to come down on its own.

## 4:30 Point 4: What's real

**ON SCREEN:** point tracker **Point 4 of 5: Real**. The board, right column, three lines appearing: **Building software got much cheaper** · **Your process can be the product** · **You can own it**

**YUSUF:**
> Now here's what's real. Building software got much cheaper and faster. What used to take a team a year, a small team can now build in weeks. AI writes a lot of the code, and people check it.
>
> For years you had two choices. You could rent a standard CRM and bend to fit it, or you could pay a fortune for a custom build. But now there's actually a third option. You get a CRM built around how you sell and deliver, and a twenty-person company can afford it. And the code belongs to you.

**ON SCREEN:** two tags, one per sentence: **Rented: their process** · **Built: your way of doing business**. Then one line, big: **Your competitor can rent the same CRM. They can't rent that.**

**YUSUF:**
> And here's the part that matters most. When you rent a CRM, you run your company on the process the vendor decided on, and changing that gets very expensive. But if your CRM is built from scratch, your way of doing business goes right into it. That's how you sell, how you treat customers, and which deals you say no to. Your competitor can rent the same CRM tomorrow. They can't rent that. It becomes your company's personality, and that's very hard to copy.

**ON SCREEN:** the same post, zoomed on **when you need a "crm software", you may opt to hire an agency to just throw one up for you**. Then two tags: **Understanding your business** · **Supporting it**. Tag: **My post · June 2023**

**YUSUF:**
> In that same post, I even used a CRM as my example. I wrote that when you need a CRM, you might just have someone build one for you, instead of researching the market to find the best one. And I said two costs wouldn't go away. Someone has to understand how your business works, and someone has to support it. So that's what you're really paying for now, and that's what to check before you hire anyone.

**ON SCREEN:** four tags, one per sentence: **Built with AI: far cheaper than a normal custom build** · **CRM is all we build** · **No price per seat** · **We build it and run it for you**

**YUSUF:**

CRM is all we build, which means we've seen most of the problems before. There's no price per seat, so your bill doesn't go up every time you hire. And you don't have to build anything yourself. We build it, and we keep it running.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **Someone who runs sales at a large software company:** then **"Same features or better, at a better price. That's the value."**

**YUSUF:**

**ON SCREEN:** **DO THIS THIS WEEK: list 3 things your team does outside the CRM**. Then a card: **SAY THIS to your team: "What do you do every week outside the CRM: in a spreadsheet, a notebook or your head?"**

## 5:55 Point 5: Who should still rent

**ON SCREEN:** point tracker **Point 5 of 5: Who should still rent**. Three tags: **Under 10 people** · **Standard sales process** · **Nobody to own it**

**YUSUF:**
> So who should keep renting? If you're under ten people, a cheap standard CRM is fine. If your sales really are simple, so you find a lead, you call, you win, then a rented CRM already fits. And if nobody in your company will look after it, renting is simpler, because the vendor does that for you.
>
> Owning makes sense when your bill grows with every hire, and the work that actually matters happens in spreadsheets around the CRM.
