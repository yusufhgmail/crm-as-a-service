**ON SCREEN:** point tracker **Point 2 of 5: Seats**. HubSpot Sales Hub pricing page, zoomed: **Professional $90 per seat/month · Enterprise $150 per seat/month** (billed annually). Tag: **Prices as of Nov 2026**

## 3:10 Point 3: What's hype

**ON SCREEN:** point tracker **Point 3 of 5: Hype**. The board, left column, three lines appearing: **"Nobody will pay for software"** · **"AI builds a CRM in an afternoon"** · **"Vendors are giving up seats"**

**YUSUF:**
> Let's start with the hype. Number one is "nobody will pay for software anymore." That's just wrong. Companies will keep paying for software that does a job. The real question is the price per head.
>
> Number two, "AI builds your CRM in an afternoon." It can build a working screen in an afternoon, sure. But a CRM your business runs on for five years needs a lot more. Your data has to move properly. You need permissions and backups. And you need someone to fix it when it breaks.

**ON SCREEN:** Salesforce Agentforce pricing page, zoomed: **$2 per conversation · $500 per 100k Credits · Agentforce add-ons $125 user/month**. Tag: **Prices as of Nov 2026**
Link: https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/pricing/

**YUSUF:**
> Number three is "the vendors are dropping seats." But look at Salesforce's own AI pricing page. They charge two dollars per conversation. And there's still a price per user on top. HubSpot's plans now come with credits too. So the seat isn't gone. They're just stacking usage on top of it.

## 4:30 Point 4: What's real

**ON SCREEN:** point tracker **Point 4 of 5: Real**. The board, right column, three lines appearing: **Building software got much cheaper** · **Your process can be the product** · **You can own it**

## 5:55 Point 5: Who should still rent

**ON SCREEN:** point tracker **Point 5 of 5: Who should still rent**. Three tags: **Under 10 people** · **Standard sales process** · **Nobody to own it**

**YUSUF:**
> So who should keep renting? If you're under ten people, a cheap standard CRM is fine. If your sales really are simple, a rented CRM already fits. You find a lead, you call, you win. And if nobody in your company will look after it, renting is simpler. The vendor does that for you.
>
> Owning makes sense when the bill grows with every hire, and the work that actually matters happens in spreadsheets around the CRM.
