### Post 1 · story
This company pays twenty-six thousand dollars a year for HubSpot. They have no sales team.

It's a consulting and training company. They run webinars and conferences, and they have consulting clients. They wrote out what they use HubSpot for, and it came to four things: webinar registrations, conference registrations, consulting clients and email.

HubSpot isn't a bad product. But in their words, this is enterprise-sales-team pricing for what amounts to an email marketing tool plus a glorified list of clients and event attendees. They don't use deal pipelines or sales forecasting, none of the stuff that pricing tier seems to assume you need.

That's the test, and it isn't about price. Forty thousand a year can be a great deal, or a terrible one. It depends on one thing: does the CRM do the jobs your business needs, the way your business does them?

They're paying for the way HubSpot thinks a company sells.

Write down the jobs your CRM must do. Then put your whole bill next to that list.

What's on your list?

### Post 2 · how-to
Go to Settings, Users and Teams, and open the Seats tab.

HubSpot doesn't have one kind of seat. There are Core seats, Sales seats, Service seats and Revenue seats. And some everyday jobs need a specific seat.

Take quotes. HubSpot's own help page says you must have a Revenue Hub seat to be able to create quotes. One company wrote that they already pay around fifteen thousand euros a year, and they say their bill is going to twenty-five thousand. For the most basic step in their sales process.

So write down every seat type you pay for, how many of each, and the price per seat from your invoice.

Then do the same for the hubs. Go to Account and Billing, then Subscriptions. List every hub you pay for, and next to each one, the one job it does for your business this month.

One founder signed a three-year contract for five hubs. Three of the five were never switched on. They asked to change the contract, and HubSpot refused.

Every hub you pay for should do a job. If you can't say what it is, why are you paying?

Which hub on your list would you struggle to name a job for?

### Post 3 · opinion
Trimming your HubSpot bill is not the same as fixing it.

Lines one and two, the seats and the hubs, you can trim. Drop a hub nobody uses. Get the seat types right. That's real money, and you should do it.
