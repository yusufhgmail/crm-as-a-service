## Leak 1: Seats nobody uses

Leak number one: seats. A seat is one person's login, and HubSpot charges you for every one. Usually you buy seats for everyone at the start, and then nobody ever removes them.

This company asked about it on HubSpot's forum in February. They're paying for full seats for people who, in their own words, don't really work in HubSpot at all. They work in Asana and Outlook. But their emails still have to get logged in HubSpot, and a free seat can't do that. So they're stuck paying. And HubSpot's own community manager confirmed it.

And this one is from August. A company brought in developers for a four-week project, and they had to buy extra paid seats just so the developers could look at the data.

In our study, more than a hundred HubSpot customers complained about what they pay per seat.

So here's the check. Go to Settings, Users and Teams, open the Seats tab, and look at the last active dates. Anyone who hasn't logged in for thirty days is a seat you're paying for and nobody's using. Write that number down. You might not be able to remove them until your renewal, and that's leak number four.

## Leak 2: The contact meter

Leak number two: the contact meter. If you have Marketing Hub, you also pay for marketing contacts. That's everyone you're allowed to send marketing emails to.

Marketing Hub Professional starts at about eight hundred dollars a month, and that includes two thousand marketing contacts. If you go over, you pay more. And here's the problem. Contacts end up as marketing contacts without anyone meaning to. Someone does an import. A form comes in. Another tool syncs over. And now your old customers and your suppliers are on the meter.

Here's one from Reddit. They imported their contacts, and it switched them over to their marketing contacts budget.

So the check. Filter your contacts by marketing status. Anyone you haven't emailed in six months, set them to non-marketing. They stay in your CRM. Now, HubSpot won't lower your bill today, and you can usually only drop a tier at renewal. But it stops the meter from pushing you up another tier. Write that down for leak four.

So that's leaks one and two. What you pay for people, and what you pay for contacts.

## Leak 3: The tier jump

Leak number three: the tier jump. Let's say you're on Starter, and you need one thing. Custom reports, a bigger automation, more pipelines. And that one thing is only on the next tier. So you're not paying for one feature, you're paying for the whole next level.

Here are HubSpot's prices today. Sales Hub Starter is twenty dollars a seat. Professional is up to a hundred dollars a seat, you have to commit for a year, and there's a fifteen-hundred-dollar onboarding fee you can't skip. So a ten-person team goes from two hundred dollars a month to a thousand, plus fifteen hundred up front. That's five times the price, for the one feature you needed.

One company on Reddit put it like this: can I not have half these features and pay fifty dollars? The jump from one plan to another is nuts.

More than three hundred HubSpot customers in our study complained about this jump. After price itself, it's the biggest money complaint we found.

So here's the check. Write down the one feature that made you go up a tier. Then ask your team, if we went back down, what would we actually lose? If it's one report or one automation, there's usually a cheaper way to get it.

And sometimes what you need isn't on any tier at all. That's leak five.

## Leak 4: The renewal

Leak number four: the renewal. Remember the reviewer from the start? This is where it happened. So let's look at what HubSpot's own terms say.

Your subscription renews automatically, for up to a year. If you want to stop it, you have to turn off auto-renew in Account and Billing before your term ends. HubSpot can raise you to full list price with thirty days' notice. That notice is one email, and for the reviewer from the start, it got lost among all the sales emails. And you can't downgrade until renewal. So if you got a big discount the first year, and people in our study talk about sixty percent off, year two can look very different.

So the check. Open Account and Billing, go to Subscriptions, and write down three things. Number one, your renewal date. Number two, is auto-renew still on. Number three, what you'll pay when the discount ends. Then put a reminder in your calendar sixty days before the renewal. That's when you negotiate, and that's when you remove the seats from leak one.

So that's leaks three and four. What the next tier costs you, and what the renewal does to your price.

## Leak 5: The workarounds

Leak number five, and the biggest one. It never shows up on your HubSpot invoice. It's everything HubSpot can't do for your business, and what your team does instead.

Here's the thing. HubSpot was built for one kind of company. And a roofer, a freight company, a distributor, they don't sell like that. You've got your own pricing rules. You've got jobs and inspections and projects. You've got your own accounting system. So when HubSpot can't do something, one of two things happens.

Either you pay to bend it. A consultant for every change, Zapier to connect your other tools, an add-on for quotes.

Or you just accept it. A spreadsheet next to the CRM. The same manual step every day, done by the same people. Data that only lives in someone's inbox. And after a while nobody even questions it. It's just how things work here.

In our study, almost four hundred HubSpot customers complained that it just doesn't fit how they work. That's more than the ones who complained about seats, or about renewals.

So here's the check. Ask your team one question. What do you do outside HubSpot every week, because HubSpot can't do it? Write down every spreadsheet, every manual step, everything they type in twice. Then add up twelve months of consultants, Zapier and add-ons. That list, and that number, that's what HubSpot really costs you.
