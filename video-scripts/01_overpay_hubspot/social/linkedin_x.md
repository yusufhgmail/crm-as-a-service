### Post 1 · story
This reviewer says they saw "Cancel Anytime". It turned out to be a three-year lock-in.

I've set up CRMs for more than 450 companies, and I'll say this first: HubSpot isn't a bad product. For a lot of companies it's the right one.

But this reviewer says the price increase came in one email, buried under all the sales emails. Now they're locked in for another year. Another company says they were paying around seventy thousand a year, and still got a hard time at renewal when they tried to downgrade.

None of this is hidden. HubSpot's own terms say it:
- your subscription renews automatically, for up to a year
- they can raise you to full list price with 30 days' notice
- you can't downgrade until renewal

The terms are clear. The inbox is where it gets lost.

So open Account and Billing, then Subscriptions, and write down three things. Your renewal date. Whether auto-renew is still on. And what you'll pay when the first-year discount ends. Then put a reminder in your calendar 60 days before. That's when you negotiate.

When is your renewal date, and does anyone besides you know it?

### Post 2 · how-to
I've set up CRMs for more than 450 companies. Usually you buy seats for everyone at the start, and then nobody ever removes them.

That's not stupid. When you set up a CRM, you want everyone in it.

But go to Settings, Users and Teams, and open the Seats tab. Look at the last active dates. Anyone who hasn't logged in for 30 days is a seat you're paying for and nobody's using.

It's not always unused logins, though. One company on HubSpot's forum pays for full seats for people who, in their own words, don't really work in HubSpot at all. They work in Asana and Outlook. But their emails still have to get logged in HubSpot, and a free seat can't do that. Another brought in developers for a four-week project, and had to buy extra paid seats just so the developers could look at the data.

In our study of what more than 1,400 HubSpot customers say about their bills, more than a hundred complained about what they pay per seat.

Write your number down. You might not be able to remove those seats until your renewal. But you'll know exactly what to cut when it comes.

How many seats on your account haven't logged in for 30 days?

### Post 3 · opinion
Every HubSpot problem is one of three things: a setting, a tier, or a fit problem.

A setting, you can fix today. Unused seats and marketing contacts are settings. A tier, you negotiate at renewal.

Fit is different. You can't fix it inside HubSpot. There's no setting and no tier that makes a CRM work the way your business works.

HubSpot was built for one kind of company, and that's not a criticism. But a roofer, a freight company or a distributor doesn't sell like that. They've got their own pricing rules, jobs, inspections, their own accounting system.

So they pay to bend it. A consultant for every change, Zapier, an add-on for quotes. Or they accept it. A spreadsheet next to the CRM, and nobody questions it anymore.

In our study, almost four hundred HubSpot customers complained that it just doesn't fit how they work. That's more than complained about seats or renewals.

Ask your team one question: what do you do outside HubSpot every week, because HubSpot can't do it?

### Post 4 · story
One accidental import, and a reviewer says they were locked into a higher marketing contact tier for a multi-year contract.

I've set up CRMs for more than 450 companies. Contacts end up on the meter without anyone meaning to, and old customers and suppliers end up on it too.

If you have Marketing Hub, you pay for seats and for marketing contacts. Marketing Hub Professional starts at about $800 a month, and that includes 2,000 marketing contacts. Go over, and you pay more. Then someone does an import, or a form comes in, or another tool syncs over.

On Reddit, one company says they imported their contacts and it switched them over to their marketing contacts budget. The Trustpilot reviewer says the higher tier stayed for the rest of the contract.

The check takes a few minutes. Filter your contacts by marketing status. Anyone you haven't emailed in six months, set to non-marketing. They stay in your CRM.

HubSpot won't lower your bill today, and you can usually only drop a tier at renewal. But it stops the meter from pushing you up another tier.

Do you know how many marketing contacts you pay for, and who put them there?

### Post 5 · how-to
Sales Hub Starter is $20 a seat. Professional is up to $100 a seat. For a ten-person team, that's $200 a month against $1,000.

I've set up CRMs for more than 450 companies, and this is the jump to look at. You need one thing. Custom reports, a bigger automation, more pipelines. And it's only on the next tier. So you're not paying for one feature, you're paying for the whole next level.

On HubSpot's pricing page, Professional also means a one-year commitment and a $1,500 onboarding fee you can't skip. Five times the price, for the one feature you needed.

One company on Reddit put it like this: can I not have half these features and pay $50? The jump from one plan to another is nuts.

In our study, more than 300 HubSpot customers complained about the jump. After price itself, it's the biggest money complaint we found.

Before you upgrade, write down the one feature that's pushing you there. Then ask your team: if we went back down, what would we actually lose? If it's one report or one automation, there's usually a cheaper way to get it.

What was the last feature that made you change tier?

### Post 6 · opinion
Four HubSpot partners, four quotes: 20k, 35k, 55k and 80k EUR.

A Reddit post says they talked to four HubSpot Solutions Partners. The cheapest, 20k EUR, was a templated setup, basically a wizard. That's what it can cost to bend HubSpot around a business it wasn't built for.

I'll say this first: HubSpot isn't a bad product. It was built for one kind of company. A roofer, a freight company or a distributor doesn't sell like that. They've got their own pricing rules, jobs, inspections and their own accounting system.

So when HubSpot can't do something, one of two things happens. You pay to bend it: a consultant for every change, Zapier to connect your other tools, an add-on for quotes. Or you accept it: a spreadsheet next to the CRM, the same manual step every day, data that only lives in someone's inbox.

A logistics company on X says HubSpot couldn't handle their Incoterms and bonded transit records. A small business on G2 says they still need spreadsheets for most of the important data.

So add up twelve months of consultants, Zapier and add-ons. That number, and your list of workarounds, is what HubSpot really costs you.

What did you pay last year to make your CRM do something it can't?
