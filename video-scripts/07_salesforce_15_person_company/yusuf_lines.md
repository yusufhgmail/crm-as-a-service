## Cost 1: The licence

Cost number one is the licence. Salesforce charges per person, per month, and the price depends on your edition. That's the package that decides which features you get.

Here are the prices when I recorded this, in November 2026, from Salesforce's own page. They'll change, so treat them as an example.

Starter Suite is twenty-five dollars per user per month. Pro Suite is a hundred. Core is a hundred and ninety-five. Advanced is three hundred and ninety-five, and Max is five hundred and fifty. And from Pro Suite up, you're billed annually.

If you're on Pro Suite, here's the catch, and it's in Salesforce's own table. You get five flows. A flow is an automation, like when a deal is won, a job gets created. So you get five of those for the whole company. You get no approval processes. And the API, which is how Salesforce talks to your accounting, costs twenty-five dollars more per user per month.

So let's do the maths for fifteen people. Pro Suite is eighteen thousand dollars a year. Add the API, and it's twenty-two thousand five hundred. Now say you need a sixth automation, or an approval step. That's Core, which is thirty-five thousand one hundred. That's almost double Pro Suite, for the same fifteen people.

So count the automations your business really needs. If it's more than five, now you know what your next quote looks like.

## Cost 2: The add-ons

Cost number two is the add-ons. What's on the price page is just where the bill starts. And Salesforce actually says so themselves, in their own FAQ.

Back to our twelve-person firm. They needed a dialer, call recording, accounting sync, an AI tool and forms. The forms tool alone was quoted at four thousand eight hundred dollars a year. And every one of those is a separate vendor with its own twelve-month lock-in.

And watch the contract. One customer found Premier Success, which is a paid support plan, in their renewal. They took it out, and the rep put it back. That happened six times. Removing it saved them thirty thousand dollars.

So read every line of your order form. If there's anything you didn't ask for, ask why it's there.

## Cost 3: The people

Cost number three is the people. Salesforce can do almost anything, but somebody has to set it up, and somebody has to change it. A small company usually doesn't have an admin, so you pay a consultant by the hour.

Take this thirty-person firm. They moved from Salesforce to Zoho, and they wrote about it in September 2026. On Salesforce, every small change needed a consultant. That was two hundred dollars an hour, and three weeks of waiting.

In our study, around two hundred and eighty Salesforce customers complained that it's too complicated, and about a hundred and twenty complained about paying a consultant for every change.

So add that up for your own company. Take twelve months of consultant invoices, and add the hours your own people spend fixing Salesforce. But the bigger cost is actually the three weeks. Every time your business needs to change something, it just stands still and waits.

## Cost 4: The renewal

Cost number four is the renewal. This is Salesforce's own main services agreement, updated in October 2026. Unless your order form says otherwise, you renew automatically for another year. If you want to stop it, you have to give written notice at least thirty days before the term ends, and email counts. And unless your order form says otherwise, you can't reduce your licences during the term.

And look at this sentence. If you renew with fewer licences or a shorter term, they re-price you, without regard to what you paid per licence before. So if you buy less, each licence can cost more.

That's what happened to one customer. They paid about a hundred thousand a year, they cut their licences in half, and everything went to list price.

More than a hundred and fifty Salesforce customers in our study complained about renewals and contracts.

So find your renewal date. Click the Setup gear, open Your Account, then Contracts. Set a reminder for ninety days before, and on that day, email your rep what you want to change. The contract says thirty days, but give yourself ninety.

## Cost 5: The fit, and the way out

Cost number five is the fit. For a small company, Salesforce is too much and not enough at the same time. You're paying for a platform built for thousands of users, and your own way of working still doesn't fit, unless you pay a consultant.

Our twelve-person firm said it best. Their Salesforce is built around how their business runs, so they can't easily leave. So what are your options? There are three real ways out.

Number one is a smaller Salesforce. That means fewer licences, fewer add-ons and a lower edition. But you just saw that renewal clause. If you cut licences at renewal, they re-price you, and each licence can cost more.

Number two is another rented CRM, like HubSpot, Zoho or Pipedrive. One eighteen-person company moved in 2025. It took four months and cost them about forty thousand dollars. And their yearly CRM bill went from ninety thousand to under twenty-five.

But in our study, about one in five Salesforce customers who moved to another rented CRM regretted it.

Number three is software you own, built only for what your business does. One company pays about a hundred thousand a year for fifty licences, and they're building their own for less than half of that, hosting included. They're still building.

Now, here's the honest risk. Someone who has built a custom CRM themselves warned a small-company owner in the same spot. They said don't hire one engineer to build it and maintain it, because if that person leaves, nobody knows how it works. Building it is the easy part. Keeping it running is the hard part.

Before you pick any way out, ask your team one question. What do we do outside Salesforce every week, because it can't do it, or because changing it costs a consultant? Write all of that down, because that list is what any new system has to do on day one. And run a data export this month. When the contract ends, you have to ask for your data within thirty days.
