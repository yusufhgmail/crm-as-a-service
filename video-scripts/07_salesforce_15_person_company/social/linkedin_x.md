The forms tool alone was quoted at four thousand eight hundred a year, and every one of those is a separate vendor with its own twelve-month lock-in.

The owner described their Salesforce as a system of record built around their specific operating model. That's why they can't just leave. It's also why the price keeps going up.

For a small company, Salesforce is too much and not enough at the same time. You pay for a platform built for thousands of users. And your own way of working still doesn't fit, unless you pay a consultant.

What does your team do outside Salesforce every week?

### Post 2 · how-to
Me and my team have set up CRMs for hundreds of companies. Here's the Salesforce renewal check I'd do this week.

Click the Setup gear, open Your Account, then Contracts. Write down your renewal date and whether it auto-renews.

Set a reminder ninety days before. That day, email your rep what you want to change, and keep every reply. The contract says thirty days. Give yourself ninety.

None of this is hidden. It's all in Salesforce's own main services agreement:
- you renew automatically for another year
- to stop it, written notice at least thirty days before the term ends, and email counts
- you can't reduce your licences during the term, unless your order form says otherwise
- renew with fewer licences or a shorter term, and they re-price you, without regard to what you paid before

One customer, at a hundred thousand a year, halved their licence count and said everything went to list price.

Then open Setup, Company Information. Write down your edition, the licences you pay for, and the ones actually used. And list every app and add-on around your Salesforce, with its price and renewal date.

Read every line of your order form. Anything you didn't ask for, ask why it's there.

When is your next renewal?
