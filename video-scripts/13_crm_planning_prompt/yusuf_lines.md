## The prompt

The prompt's in the description, word for word, so you can copy it.

## Part 1: Who you are

Say you run a heat pump company with twenty-five people. The prompt has six parts. In the first one, you fill in what your company does and how big it is. It also tells the AI not to recommend a specific CRM, because otherwise you'll probably just get a list of products.

## Part 2: How you sell

Next, you tell it how you sell, the way it really works on a normal day. Write at least a page on this, because three lines gets you a generic plan. So for the heat pump company, you'd write that it makes thirty quotes a month, and about half need a second visit to the customer's home. And you'd write that nobody follows up on quotes that don't get signed.

## Part 3: How you deliver

Then you tell it what happens after the deal is signed, until you're paid, and what comes after that. Most CRMs stop when the deal is won, so people leave this out. The heat pump company owes every customer a service visit after twelve months, and they often forget.

Don't write this part yourself. Ask the person who does your invoicing, because they know what really happens. Say this. "Can you write down every step from a signed deal until we're paid, and what we owe the customer after that? I mean the way it really happens, with the spreadsheets and the things you chase by hand".

## Part 4: The rules

I think the most important line in the prompt is the one that makes the AI ask you questions first. If you don't tell it to, it just guesses, and it sounds sure of itself.

When I tried this, it asked why those quotes need a second visit. The answer is that nobody checks the electrics on the first visit. That's fifteen extra trips a month.

## Part 5: What you want back

Then the AI writes the plan, and it starts with where you're losing money. The heat pump company makes thirty quotes a month, and eighteen get signed. That leaves twelve, and nobody follows up on those. At fifteen thousand dollars a job, that's a hundred and eighty thousand dollars a month. You won't win all of them, but win two and that's thirty thousand dollars. At your own company, count last month's quotes that nobody followed up on, and multiply that by your average deal. That number is the first thing your CRM has to fix.

The plan also gives you two pipelines, one for selling and one for delivery, with a rule for each stage. A quote can't go out until someone has checked the electrics and where the outdoor unit goes, and that should remove most of those fifteen extra trips.

And it tells you what to automate. To fix those twelve quotes, it says that when a quote has gone three days without a signature, the salesperson gets a follow-up task.

## Part 6: The checklist

You also get a checklist for before you pay anyone. It starts with who owns your data, and how you get it out if you leave.

When a seller says getting your data out is easy, I wouldn't just take their word for it. Ask them to export one customer, with the notes and the files, before you sign.

## Cut the plan down

Every step in that plan is admin your team has to do, so before you use it, ask the AI what you can cut. In my experience, that's the main question with any CRM. Just paste this in. "What can we cut from this plan and still run the business with as little admin as possible"?

Then send the plan to two people on your team, and ask them this. "This is a first draft of how we'd run our CRM. What's missing, and which step would you never actually do"?

## Buy or build

The plan also tells you what a standard CRM won't do for you. If it only misses a few small things, buy a standard CRM. If the part it can't do is what wins you deals, build one around your plan.

If you're buying, here's the email I promised. It's in the description too. Send it to every vendor before the demo. "Here's our plan. In the demo, please run one real job of ours, from first contact to final payment. Show us which parts your CRM can't do. And tell us in writing what it costs at twice our size". If they can't run that job without workarounds, that CRM doesn't fit.
