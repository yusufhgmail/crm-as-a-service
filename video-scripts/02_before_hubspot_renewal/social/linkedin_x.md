## LinkedIn text posts

### Post 1 · story
A small company on Sales Hub Professional had a plan, and it wasn't a bad one.

They'd had staff changes, so a few paid seats were sitting empty. They wanted to drop them at the renewal.

HubSpot's reminder emails landed in spam. The contract auto-renewed on the twenty-sixth of February. About five thousand dollars for a year, for seats nobody uses. They wrote that this was a substantial and unacceptable financial burden. And HubSpot's answer was no. The earliest change is February 2027.

HubSpot's own terms say this out loud:
- your subscription renews automatically, for up to a year
- you can't cancel in the middle of a term
- the only way to stop the renewal is to switch off auto-renew in Account and Billing

So step one takes two minutes. Open Account and Billing, then Subscriptions, then Auto-Renewal Terms. The button says "Cancel auto-renewal". You need to be a super admin or billing admin.

It doesn't cancel anything today. You keep paying until your term ends, and your account keeps working. It just means nothing renews until you've decided.

Do you know who in your company can press that button?

### Post 2 · how-to
Ninety days before your HubSpot renewal, send one email.

Subject: Our renewal on [date]. Then three lines:

1. Please send a line-item quote for renewal.
2. Remove: seats, marketing contacts, features we don't use.
3. Confirm the price for the next term in writing.

Why so early? One company was paying HubSpot about fourteen hundred and fifty pounds a month. HubSpot advised them to cut their marketing contacts before the February renewal. They did it. It renewed at the old tier anyway, because a separate quote had to be activated ten days before the renewal date. They got no credit and no adjustment, and were told to wait twelve months.

HubSpot can also raise you to full list price with thirty days' notice. So you want next year's price in writing long before that.

Use your numbers from step two: who logged in in the last thirty days, who you emailed in the last six months, and which features you used this year.

Keep every reply. If something changes on a phone call, ask them to confirm it by email. Then you have it in writing if anything goes wrong.

Who in your company would send this email today?

### Post 3 · opinion
Getting a better HubSpot renewal is not the same as fixing the problem.

The four steps are good ones, and you should do them all. Switch off auto-renew. Count what you use. Send the email ninety days out. Test your export.

In the end they renewed at Enterprise anyway, for $20,000. Then one Monday morning they were locked out of their own CRM. Three days with no access, and they said that meant zero production.

I've set up CRMs for more than 450 companies, and the lockout is the part to read twice. For those three days they couldn't even see if a customer had written to them. That's what happens when your data only lives inside someone else's system.

### Long post 1

Your HubSpot renewal is coming up.

Here are 5 steps, in the order you do them. The first takes two minutes.

Switch off auto-renew. HubSpot's terms say it renews automatically for up to a year, with no cancelling mid-term. Account and Billing, Subscriptions, Auto-Renewal Terms, "Cancel auto-renewal". Your account keeps working.

Then count what you use. Seats: who logged in in the last 30 days? Marketing contacts: who did you email in 6 months? Features: what did your team really use? You can't downgrade until renewal.

Start 90 days early, in writing. Email HubSpot. Ask for a quote line by line, list what you want removed, get next year's price confirmed. Keep every reply.

Get your data out. Export contacts and deals with all properties. Open the file. Notes, calls and emails aren't in a records export, so that history needs another method.

Last, ask the fit question. What do we do outside HubSpot every week because it can't do it?

In our study, around 400 HubSpot customers said it doesn't fit how they work.

Which step do you skip?

### Long post 2

A company was paying HubSpot about £1,450 a month.

HubSpot advised them to cut their marketing contacts before the February renewal. So they did.

Good plan. Fewer contacts, lower tier.

But no. It renewed at the old tier anyway, because a separate quote had to be activated ten days before the renewal date. The answer: they got no credit and no adjustment, and had to wait twelve months.

That's what happens when nothing is in writing early. Waiting twelve months to fix a thing you were told to do is nuts.

So start ninety days out. Email HubSpot and ask for a line-item quote. List exactly what you want removed. Ask them to confirm next year's price in writing. HubSpot can raise you to full list price with 30 days' notice, so you want that number way before then.

If something changes on a phone call, ask them to confirm it by email.

Anybody been told to do something before a renewal and then still got billed?

### Long post 3

HubSpot's help page is clear about what a records export holds.

A records export is your property values and your associations. That's it.

So you can export contacts, companies and deals on every HubSpot plan, with all the fields on them, like phone number or deal stage, and the links between them, like which contact belongs to which company.

But your notes, your calls, your emails, the whole history with a customer? Not in that file.

For those, HubSpot points you somewhere else: one contact at a time, an activity report, or the API.

So your history needs a different method, and you want to know that now, not the week you leave.

Try it today. Export all your contacts and deals with all properties. Open the file. Then ask: if this was all we had tomorrow, could we keep working?

Has anyone actually tried this before they needed to?
