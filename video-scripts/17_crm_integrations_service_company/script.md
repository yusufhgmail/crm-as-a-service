> Say a customer calls you and asks, "Why did I get a reminder? I paid two weeks ago." You look it up. QuickBooks says paid, your CRM says they still owe you, and the app your crews use says the invoice is still open.
>
> That happens because each of those tools keeps its own copy of the customer, and the connections between them often don't do what you'd think. Those connections are called integrations, which just means two of your tools pass information to each other, so nobody has to type it.
>
> I built a CRM SaaS company, and I sold it in 2025. Me and my team set up CRMs for hundreds of companies. Integrations are the major problem on any CRM, and they're much more complex than people expect.
>

I'll show you the five integrations a service company needs, with a simple check for each one, so you can count how many fail. And at the end, I'll show you what two companies did instead of connecting all these tools.

## 1:16 Integration 1: Quotes

**ON SCREEN:** tracker: **Integration 1 of 5: Quotes**. On the board, **CRM** in the middle and a **Quotes** box beside it, with the arrow between them. Tags: **Signed → deal won** · **Nobody retypes it**

**YUSUF:**
> The first integration connects your CRM to the tool you send quotes with. When a customer signs a quote, your CRM should mark that deal as won by itself. And nobody should have to type in what they signed.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **Example: a company with its own quoting software** (no name). Then a chain: **CRM ↔ quoting software**. Then in red: **Connection fails → no quotes go out**. Then tag: **Built around what they needed**

**YUSUF:**
> The broken connection I remember best was at a company that sent many quotes every day, to restaurants and other places. Their CRM couldn't handle their quoting rules, so they built separate quoting software and connected it to the CRM. When that connection failed, they just couldn't send quotes. And even when it worked, it was super messy. Much later, my old company built a CRM around what they needed.

**ON SCREEN:** **DO THIS: last signed quote vs CRM deal · do they match?** Two cards side by side: **Signed quote · $?** · **CRM deal · $?**. Tag: **Doesn't match, or someone typed it = not connected**

**YUSUF:**
> To check your own quotes, compare your last signed quote with the deal in your CRM. If the total or the items don't match, or someone had to type them in, this connection isn't working.

## 2:11 Integration 2: Jobs

**ON SCREEN:** tracker: **Integration 2 of 5: Jobs**. On the board, a **Jobs** box joins the CRM. Tags: **Won → job** · **Extras → invoice** · **Jobber · ServiceTitan · Housecall Pro**

**YUSUF:**
> The second integration connects your CRM to the app your crews use for their jobs, like Jobber or ServiceTitan. When a deal is won, it should become a job without anyone copying it over. And when the job's done, anything extra the crew did should go onto the invoice. If a crew does an extra hour and it stays on paper, you did that hour for free.

**ON SCREEN:** **DO THIS: last 20 jobs · what the crew wrote vs what went on the invoice** and a table: **Job · Crew wrote · On the invoice**. Tag: **Never billed, or copied by hand = not working**

**YUSUF:**
> To check this one, take your last twenty jobs, and put what the crew wrote down next to what went on the invoice. Add up what never got billed. If anything's missing, or someone had to copy the job over by hand, this connection isn't working.

## 2:55 Integration 3: Accounting (QuickBooks)

**ON SCREEN:** tracker: **Integration 3 of 5: Accounting**. On the board, a **QuickBooks** box joins the CRM, two arrows. Out of the CRM: **Job done → invoice, same items**. Back in: **Paid / overdue**

**YUSUF:**
> The third integration connects your CRM to your accounting, and for a lot of service companies that's QuickBooks. When a job is done, the invoice should land in QuickBooks, and when the customer pays, your CRM should know.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **Typing saved: minor**. Then tags as he says them: **What they bought** · **What they've paid** · **Which marketing brought the money**

**YUSUF:**
> The time you save on typing is minor. The real value is the full view of the customer. Your team can see what each customer bought and how much they've paid, so a salesperson knows who's worth calling. And when your CRM knows who paid, you can see which marketing actually brought in the money.

**ON SCREEN:** typed note: **CRM: deal won** → **Accounting: invoice sent · paid · not paid in full**

**YUSUF:**
> This connection is hard to get right. In the CRM, you close the deal, and it's won. But accounting has a bunch of stages. You sent the invoice, you got paid, you didn't get paid the full amount. So these systems have completely different ways of seeing the world, and things get lost between them.

**ON SCREEN:** HubSpot help-center screenshot, "Connect HubSpot and QuickBooks Online", red box on **"Payments associated with multiple invoices don't sync to HubSpot."** · tag **1 payment, 3 invoices = still looks unpaid** · tag **Updated Jul 2026** · source tag **HubSpot help center · checked Nov 2026**
Link: https://knowledge.hubspot.com/integrations/connect-hubspot-and-quickbooks-online

**YUSUF:**
> For example, when I recorded this, HubSpot's help page said that if one payment covers several invoices, that payment doesn't reach HubSpot. So HubSpot can still show those invoices as unpaid.

**ON SCREEN:** Jobber help-center screenshot, "QuickBooks Integration FAQs: NEW QuickBooks Integration", red boxes on **"The sync is now one way, from Jobber to QuickBooks."** · **"“Paid” status on invoices no longer sync back to Jobber."** · tag **Updated Oct 2026** · source tag **Jobber help center · checked Nov 2026**
Link: https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/12642621815831-QuickBooks-Integration-FAQs-NEW-QuickBooks-Integration

**YUSUF:**
> And Jobber's help page said its connection to QuickBooks only runs one way, into QuickBooks. So when an invoice gets paid in QuickBooks, Jobber doesn't find out. That's how a customer who already paid gets a reminder, and calls you to ask why.

**ON SCREEN:** **DO THIS: last 5 invoices · paid shows in your CRM?** A table on the board: **Invoice · Paid in CRM?**, five empty rows. Then card: **SAY THIS (to whoever set it up):** "Which way does our QuickBooks connection run? And what happens when one payment covers several invoices?"

**YUSUF:**
> To check this one, take your last five invoices. Can you see in your CRM if each one's paid, without opening QuickBooks? If you can't, this connection isn't working. So ask whoever set it up, "Which way does our QuickBooks connection run? And what happens when one payment covers several invoices?"

## 4:40 Integration 4: Email and calendar

**ON SCREEN:** tracker: **Integration 4 of 5: Email + calendar**. On the board, an **Email + calendar** box joins the CRM. Tag: **Every email + meeting → customer record**. Then tag: **Lives in 1 person's inbox = leaves when they leave**

**YUSUF:**
> The fourth integration connects your CRM to your email and calendar. Every customer email and meeting should land on that customer's record in the CRM, so the whole team can see what's been said. If that history only lives in one person's inbox, then when that salesperson leaves, the history leaves with them.

**ON SCREEN:** card: **SAY THIS (to one of your salespeople):** "Can you open your last three customer meetings in the CRM with me?" Tag: **Any missing = not working**

**YUSUF:**
> To check this one, sit down with one of your salespeople. Just say, "Can you open your last three customer meetings in the CRM with me?" If any are missing, this connection isn't working.

## 5:13 Integration 5: Everything else

**ON SCREEN:** tracker: **Integration 5 of 5: Everything else**. On the board, a last box, **Zapier / a person**, with arrows to every other box. Tag: **Zapier = passes information between apps**

**YUSUF:**
> The fifth integration is everything else, all the gaps between your tools. Some companies fill those gaps with a tool like Zapier, which passes information from one app to another. And some have someone on the team copying things from one screen to another.

**ON SCREEN:** Zapier help-center screenshot, "How to troubleshoot errors in Zap workflows", red box on **"Your Zap will automatically turn off if 95% of its runs result in errors in the last 7 days."** · tag **Zap = 1 automation in Zapier** · source tag **Zapier help center · checked Nov 2026**
Link: https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496037690637-How-to-troubleshoot-errors-in-Zap-workflows

**YUSUF:**
> Those Zapier connections can just stop. Zapier's help page said a connection turns itself off if it fails almost every time for a week.

**ON SCREEN:** **DO THIS: any Zapier connections stopped?** Card: **ASK YOUR TEAM:** do you spend hours every week copying things by hand? Tag: **Yes to either = not working**

**YUSUF:**
> So to check this one, ask whoever set up your Zapier if any connections have stopped. Then ask your team if they spend hours every week copying things by hand. If the answer to either is yes, count this one as not working.

## 5:56 What to do if most of them fail

**ON SCREEN:** the full board, all five boxes. Tag: **1 or 2 not working = fix those · 3 or more not working = keep watching**. Then tags as he says them: **Direction: someone else's call** · **What gets through: someone else's call**

**YUSUF:**
> If one or two of those five integrations aren't working, ask whoever set them up to fix those, and keep the tools you have. But if three or more aren't working, adding more connections probably won't solve it. The companies that make those tools decided which way the information goes and what gets through, and you can't change that with a setting. That's why it's so much more effective if you can build your own system, because then you decide how it all works.
