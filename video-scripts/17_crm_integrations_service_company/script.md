## 1:15 Integration 1: Accounting (QuickBooks)

**ON SCREEN:** tracker: **Integration 1 of 5: Accounting**. On the map, the **QuickBooks** box and two arrows. Out of the CRM: **Job won → invoice, same lines**. Back in: **Paid / overdue**. Tag: **Integrated = both arrows, nobody typing**

**YUSUF:**
> Number one is accounting, and for a lot of service companies that's QuickBooks. Integrated means a won job puts the invoice in QuickBooks with the same lines the customer agreed to, and when the customer pays, your CRM knows.

**ON SCREEN:** typed note: **2017, to a customer: "it doesn't actually save any time"**. Then **Typing saved: maybe 5% (my estimate)**. Then tags as he says them: **What they bought** · **What they've paid** · **When to renew** · **Which marketing brought the money**

**YUSUF:**
> In 2017 I told a customer that linking the CRM to accounting doesn't actually save any time, because finance still has to check the sales contacts by hand. I'd still say the typing is minor, maybe five percent.
>
> What you get is the whole customer. Your team sees what someone bought, what they've paid, and when it's time to renew, so a salesperson knows who to call. And you can see which marketing actually brought in the money.

**ON SCREEN:** typed note: **CRM: deal won** → **Accounting: invoice sent · paid · not paid in full** · tag **About 15% had trouble here (my estimate)**

**YUSUF:**
> In my experience, about fifteen percent of companies had trouble getting the two to agree. In the CRM, you close the deal, and it's won. But accounting has a bunch of stages. You sent the invoice, you got paid, you didn't get paid the full amount. These systems have completely different ways of seeing the world.

So your CRM can still show those invoices as unpaid, and that's one way you get that phone call.

## 3:50 Integration 2: Quotes and e-signature

**ON SCREEN:** tracker: **Integration 2 of 5: Quotes**. On the map, **Quotes + e-signature**. Into the CRM: **Signed → deal won, same lines**. Out: **Lines → job + invoice**. Tag: **What they signed = what you bill**

**YUSUF:**
> Secondly, quotes and e-signature. When this works, the lines the customer signed become the job and the invoice, and the deal moves to won by itself.

**ON SCREEN:** tag: **Example: a company with its own quoting software** (no name). Then a chain, one step at a time: **Fields in the CRM → button → quoting software → approved / not approved**. Then in red: **Link fails → no quotes go out**

**YUSUF:**
> The broken integration I remember best was at a company that sent a lot of quotes every day, to restaurants and other places. Their CRM couldn't handle their quoting rules, so they built their own quoting software and connected the two. You filled in a pile of fields, clicked a button, and a field came back saying approved or not approved. When that link failed, they just couldn't send quotes. And even when it worked, it was super messy. My old company took them on much later, and built a CRM around what they needed.

**ON SCREEN:** **DO THIS: last signed quote vs CRM deal vs invoice · all 3 match?** Three cards side by side: **Signed quote · $?** · **CRM deal · $?** · **Invoice · $?**

**YUSUF:**
> Put your last signed quote next to its CRM deal and its invoice. Is it the same total and the same lines? And did anybody have to type something to make them match?
