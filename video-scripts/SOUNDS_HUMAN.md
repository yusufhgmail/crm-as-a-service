Title: How a script should sound

[All video scripts](README.md) · [Script rules](SCRIPT_RULES.md) · [Process and scoring](CLAUDE.md)

Yusuf's standard is a human talking from the heart, with no scripted teleprompter. Every sentence has a point, follows from the one before and gets understood the first time, in simple, direct language.

A viewer hears each line once, at his speed, and can't go back. So a script has to be written for the ear. The first part of this file says how. The second part lists what gives AI away. The Human and Easy to understand scores in the [Optimized process](CLAUDE.md) use both.

A script can pass every rule here and still sound prepared. So always finish by reading it aloud from start to finish, and ask if he'd say this to a friend because he cares about it.

# Write for the ear

## Start from how he talks

**Start from his words.** Yusuf says simple ideas better than the script does, and his retakes usually beat it. So before you write a line, check if he's already said it. His words are in David's repo, in `original_sources/`, `videos/YUSUF_SPEECH_LESSONS.md` and `survey/yusuf_videos/VOICE_GUIDE.md`. If he has said it, use his words or their shape. If he hasn't, say the point aloud the way you'd tell a friend and write that down. Then read it back and ask if it sounds like him.

**Talk to one person.** Picture one CEO across the table, and say "you" and "I". People learn more from a conversational lesson than from a formal one.

**Leave room for him.** Write short, plain claims and don't polish them, because he'll say them his way. Don't script every reaction either. After a story he may react, or ask "Has that ever happened to you?" Once or twice in a video is enough, because more sounds like a rant.

**Never stage it.** Don't write in "um", false starts or stumbles, and don't add doubt he doesn't have. Listeners rate a stumbling speaker as less competent and less sure.

## Say one thing at a time

**Use full spoken sentences.** Shorter never means telegram style. When a line is telegram style, he stops and says the full reasoning, and his version is longer and clearer. Join sentences with "so", "and", "but" and "because", since that's how people reason aloud, and never remove them to make a line shorter or lower a count. Use contractions like "it's", "don't" and "you'll". No colon reveals, no slogans, and no fragments standing in for sentences.

- Not like this: "In 2018: a hundred and twenty people, thirteen million dollars in revenue. Twenty fewer people, six million more."
- But like this: "In 2018 they had a hundred and twenty people and thirteen million dollars in revenue. That's twenty fewer people and six million dollars more."

**One idea in a sentence.** If a sentence goes two ways, make it two sentences. Lists of three or four clauses trip him most, and one took eight tries. Keep most sentences near his natural length of about thirteen words, and let one run longer when he's telling a story.

- Not like this: "Write down who uses it, what it costs and when it renews."
- But like this: "Write down who uses it. Then write down what it costs and when it renews."

**Say who does what, in that order.** Start with who's acting, and put what they do right after it. A side note between the two makes the viewer hold the start of the sentence until the verb arrives.

- Not like this: "The salesperson, who already has a quota to hit and forty other things to do, won't fill in the field."
- But like this: "The salesperson won't fill in the field. They already have a quota to hit."

**Use verbs, not nouns made from verbs.** People say what someone does. Written text turns that into a thing, and AI text does it most of all.

- Not like this: "Customization of the CRM requires ongoing maintenance."
- But like this: "Every time you change the CRM, someone has to keep that change working."

**Use the everyday word.** Say "the car", never "the vehicle". Say "if", never "whether". Needless big words make people rate you as less smart. If you need a CRM term, say what it means in the same breath.

- Like this: "Those connections are called integrations, which just means two tools pass information to each other."

**Aim for one number in a sentence, and never more than two.** Write numbers in words, the way he says them: "sixty days", "forty thousand dollars". Numbers go wrong under pressure, and the ear can't hold several. Round them unless the exact figure is the point.

- Not like this: "Fifty deals at fifteen thousand each, ten deals, a hundred and fifty thousand."
- But like this: "Say you close ten deals. Each one's worth fifteen thousand dollars. That's a hundred and fifty thousand."

**Put what the ear can't hold on screen.** Menu paths trip him every time, and long lists of figures do the same. Show them, and give him a short line.

- Not like this: "Settings, Users and Teams, open the Seats tab."
- But like this: "Go to your seats page." with the path on screen.

## Lead each line into the next

**Every sentence needs a point, and it has to follow from the one before.** Yusuf, recording video 11: "every single thing that we say has a point and is coherent and fits with everything else". Start each sentence on something the viewer just heard, and end it on what's new. Say each step of the reasoning, and state the conclusion. Cut any story, line or section you can't give a reason for. Sentences that are each fine but don't lead to each other are a sign of AI. People explain: this, so that, because of this other thing.

- Not like this: "Contacts move easily when you leave. Years of notes, files, activities and custom records don't. And if your work doesn't fit anybody's screens, you can switch to a new logo and just hit the same wall."
- But like this: "You can move your contacts, but that doesn't mean you can move everything that's important. There may be years of notes and files you can't move as easily. So it's very important to pick the right CRM before you commit all of that to it."

**Never point at something the viewer hasn't heard.** Say each new line aloud after the one before it. If a line needs a fact, say the fact first. If the viewer would have to stop and work out what you mean, rewrite it.

- Not like this: "So if you walk in without your own list, you end up using the seller's."
- But like this: "So before you talk to a seller, write down what matters most to your company. If you don't, you'll end up judging the CRM by what the seller wants to show you."
- Not like this: "I built a CRM SaaS company, and I sold it. And we didn't stop at HubSpot."
- But like this: "My last videos were about HubSpot. For this one, we went wider."

**Don't lean on labels.** Never assume the viewer remembers a word you set up earlier, like "moment", "route" or "part". Each new section starts by saying what it's about in full, as if someone had just started listening.

- Not like this: "The first moment is when you want your CRM to work a little differently."
- But like this: "Renting a CRM starts to hurt when you want it to work a little differently."

**Let every line stand alone.** A line that points back trips him, and it trips the viewer too. Name the thing instead of saying "that" or "yours", and say the whole phrase: "owning your own CRM", never just "owning".

- Not like this: "And if your team's using the CRM after those fixes, you don't need us."
- But like this: "If your team is already using the CRM that you have today, then you don't need us."
- Not like this: "If that's you, and you're also running the company, I'd stick to the spreadsheet."
- But like this: "If you're the one who'd do that, and you're also running your company, it's probably not the best idea."

# What gives AI away

AI picks the most likely next word. So it drifts to claims that fit any company, it keeps one comfortable rhythm, and every paragraph comes out equally polished. Yusuf asked for this list on 5 Nov 2026 after reading "And here's the part that stings." in video 10 and calling it a typical AI comment.

One of these proves nothing, and real people do most of them now and then. A pile of them is what gives a script away. Yusuf's own lines don't count against a script. Run `python3 video-scripts/ai_tells.py <script.md>` to count the patterns a tool can find, then read the script aloud for the rest.

**1. False suspense.** A line that promises a reveal, then delivers an ordinary fact. Say the fact. A hook is different, because it names something real and the video delivers it.

- Not like this: "And here's the part that stings. HubSpot's own site calls its platform all-in-one."
- But like this: "HubSpot calls itself all-in-one on its own site. Its Professional plan comes with call recording and follow-up sequences. So if you're also paying for Gong and Outreach, you've paid for those two things twice."

**2. Telling the viewer how to feel.** "That stings", "this is where it gets interesting", "that's the scary part". Give the fact and let them react. Yusuf's own reactions on camera are fine, because they're real.

**3. "Not X, it's Y."** It knocks down something nobody said, in forms like "isn't X, it's Y", "rather than" and "the real X is Y".

- Not like this: "The bigger cost isn't on the invoices at all. It's the time."
- But like this: "You also pay in time. Somebody has to keep ten tools connected."

**4. A question nobody asked, answered straight away.** "So why do teams still do it?" Just give the reason. Questions the viewer can answer about their own company are fine, and so is a "what if" that asks them to picture something better.

**5. Punchlines.** A short, quotable line closing a paragraph. People mostly stop when they're done. A plain verdict he'd really say is fine, like "That's completely unnecessary today."

- Not like this: "So instead of a box of Lego, you get the ship."
- But like this: end on the last real thing the system does.

**6. Threes.** Three items again and again, because three sounds complete. Use the number the facts have. Two is fine.

**7. Even rhythm.** Sentences of the same length, paragraphs of the same shape. Let a long one run, and let a short one be short because the point is small.

**8. The same opener.** "Now" and "Here's" at the start of sentence after sentence. His own lead-ins are fine now and then, like "Here's a check you can do today." "So", "And", "But" and "because" are different, because they carry the reasoning.

**9. Announcing instead of saying.** "There are four parts", "Let's break this down", "Here's what that looks like". Start with the thing itself. The opening is the one place to say what's coming.

**10. Saying it again.** "So that's what one bill looks like", and recaps of what the viewer heard a minute ago.

**11. Claims that fit any company.** If the sentence would be true in any video about any software, it needs a case, a number or a name, or it goes. "Many companies" and "experts say" count too. One true detail that couldn't appear in anyone else's video beats three general claims, but never add one just to add texture.

**12. Performed honesty.** "I'll admit something", "to be upfront", "honestly", or "That's one comment, and I can't check it." It sounds honest on paper and like AI out loud. An admission only sounds real if it cost something, so say what went wrong, unless it's about his old company, which always looks good.

**13. Big stakes for small things.** "Transform", "game-changing", "fundamentally", "completely new".

**14. Stock AI words.** Delve, leverage, robust, seamless, unlock, landscape, navigate, ensure, crucial, pivotal, "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", "the reality is". Say "is", never "serves as" or "acts as".

**15. Fake analogies.** "Think of it as...". Keep an analogy only if Yusuf really uses it, like the Lego box from his old sales deck.

**16. Swapping words to avoid repeating one.** People say "the CRM" five times. AI says "the CRM", "the platform", "the system", "the tool".

**17. Clever phrasing.** A neat turn of phrase nobody would say aloud. Say it the way you'd say it to a friend.

- Not like this: "Year two is nobody's job."
- But like this: "Year two can be a disaster."

**18. "-ing" add-ons.** A sentence that ends in an extra thought starting with "leaving", "making" or "giving". AI uses these far more than people do. Say "so" and finish the thought.

- Not like this: "Each tool keeps its own copy, leaving your team to guess which one's right."
- But like this: "Each tool keeps its own copy, so your team has to guess which one's right."
