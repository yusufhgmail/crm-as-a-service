## Check 1: Who keeps it running

The first thing to check is who fixes your own CRM when it breaks. And I get why you'd want to do that yourself, because it looks free. But here's a real example from Reddit. Someone at a six-person company ran a CRM on their own server, and they set up daily backups and thought that was it, and then came updates, failed backups and the odd outage. They called the post, somehow I became the CRM department.

So write down two names. Who keeps your CRM running, and who steps in when that person's gone? And if you can't write two names, you can still own your CRM and simply pay someone else to run it.

## Check 2: Write it down

The second thing to check is if someone new could actually run your CRM. I mean, if you rent a CRM, you've still got a company to call. But if you run your own CRM, what's written down is all you've got. So ask whoever built it to write one page. Like, how do I change this safely, and how do I bring it back if it dies? And if someone else runs your CRM, that page is their job, and you just keep a copy.

## Check 3: Restore a backup

The third thing to check is your CRM's backups. Because if they run every night, it feels like you're covered, but that six-person company had daily backups, and they still failed. So restore last night's backup into a test copy, and time it, because that's how long your team would sit without a CRM if it died. And if someone else runs your CRM, you just ask them how long their last restore took.

## Check 4: Give every connection an owner

The fourth thing to check is how your CRM connects to your other tools, like QuickBooks or Xero, and this is the one I'd actually worry about most. In my experience, connections are what break most, because two systems have completely different ways of seeing the world.

Here's a real example. One company built their own quoting software and connected it to their CRM, and when that connection failed, they just couldn't send quotes.

Now, when you own your CRM, you can actually ask before you connect a tool, could this just be part of the CRM? Because then there's no connection left to break. And for every connection you keep, write down who fixes it.

## Check 5: Check who can get in

The fifth thing to check is who can get into your CRM. Nick from Sandstone actually said permissions were one of the reasons his CRM didn't last. And I understand, in a small company you trust everybody. But say a salesperson is leaving, and they log in, export every customer and take that list to their next job.

So log in as your most junior user, and see if you can export every customer. And make sure two people at your company have the admin passwords, even if someone else runs your CRM, because it's your CRM.

## Check 6: Price a year, not a build

The last thing to check is what your own CRM really costs in a year. The server's actually the cheap part. The big cost is the time of whoever keeps it running. At my old company, the typical customer took about three hours of setup and support a month, but a few took twenty-six to forty.

So write down what a year of that time would cost you, and put it next to what you pay for your CRM today. You may save real money. Greg says they save thirty to fifty thousand dollars a year. But I think the best reason to own your own CRM is that you can actually change it right away. Greg says they roll out features in real time.

## Run it yourself, or hand it over

So should you run it yourself? If two people at your company can keep your CRM running, and one of them really has the time, then yes, run it yourselves, like Greg. But if it's only you, and you're also running your company, you'll probably end up like Nick. And you don't have to choose between renting a CRM and becoming the CRM department yourself.

Now picture your company a year from now. One system that you own runs everything from the first lead to the last payment. Your quotes are inside it, so there's no connection to break. Say your sales manager wants to change how quotes go out, and that change is in your CRM the same day, because you're not waiting for another company's features. And when something breaks, someone else fixes it.
