Does the free-CRM idea fit the plan?

It fits the plan, but not as the first step. The free self-builder is already step 3 of your August roadmap; the 12:42 plan moves it to step 1. Starting with the free CRM (D) pushes David's $3,000 a month from about month 5 to month 13, needs about $70k before it pays, and pits you against free plans from HubSpot, Salesforce and Zoho. Only about 4% of free CRM users ever pay.

Start as B now and decide on E at month 9. B adds a few free tools to the current agency plan. E is the same three layers as your plan in reverse order: E's first nine months are B, then the free CRM launches once about 15 builds have taught the builder, with agents after it. The two tie on score. E is worth more at a sale (about 64 Mkr against 56 Mkr at month 36) and has the stronger moat; B is simpler.

You are both right. David's few good customers should pay the bills. Your thousands of free users are worth having as marketing and as data on which features get kept, but not as the revenue. A free CRM paid for by AI agents alone (C) scores lowest.

What the market research found

FindingWhat it means here
Free CRM is crowded: at least 8 vendors have a free plan, and HubSpot, Salesforce and Zoho are free for 2–3 users.“Free” alone won't pull users. The hook has to be “a CRM built around your company in minutes”.
About 4% of free CRM users ever pay. Good self-serve freemium products convert 3–5%.10,000 free users means about 400 paying. The model gets about 3%.
New CRMs took years to reach thousands: folk had 100,000 users and 2,000 paying after about 4 years. Attio had 2,000 customers after 3 years of building.Thousands of free users comes in year 2–3, not month 3.
Free users create most support work: at Typeform they filed about 70% of tickets. A human answer costs about $13.50; an AI answer about $1.A free CRM needs AI support from day one. The model charges $0.60 a month per active free user.
The big CRMs give basic AI agents away. Salesforce put them in its free and $25 small-business plans; HubSpot charges $0.50 per solved conversation.Agents must sell a result, such as booked meetings. Small firms pay $80–180 per booked meeting, so about $150 a month is realistic.
AI sales agents lose customers fast. In a SaaStr poll, 83% got nothing from them; ex-staff said 11x lost 70–80% of customers (11x disputes this).The model assumes 8% of agent customers leave each month.
Free tools do bring traffic: Ahrefs gets about half its traffic from them, and HubSpot's Website Grader graded 4 million sites. Neil Patel's free tool fed his agency, but he wrote that sales from it fell once the agency moved to bigger clients.Free tools help an agency, but free users skew small, which is David's worry.
Hormozi: rich customers have the same problems but pay much more to solve them, so charge 5–10× more per tier.Supports David: earn from few good clients and use free things only as marketing.
Private software companies sell near 5× yearly revenue. Small agencies sell for 2–4× owner earnings, larger ones 4–8× profit.Agent revenue raises the sale value more per dollar than agency revenue, but only once it is real.
AI coding tools mostly replace $49-a-month tools today, not CRMs. Lovable already makes $300M+ a year and is moving up.Building a CRM gets cheaper for everyone. What stays yours is knowing what to build: the learning from builds.
How the model works (assumptions)
  • Agency figures are the same in every shape that has one: a $3,000 proof, then $1,500 a month. 2.5% of clients leave each month.
  • Build work (including your own hours at an internal rate), AI and hosting cost 60% of agency revenue at first and 40% from month 19, the plan's 60% margin target.
  • New clients from content: 1 a month from month 3, 2 from month 7, 3 from month 13, 4 from month 25. Free tools add 1 a month from month 5.
  • Delivery capacity caps new clients at 2 a month at first, then 3 from month 7, 4 from month 13 and 6 from month 25. In E, this cap stops many free-CRM leads from becoming clients.
  • A free CRM costs $4,000 a month in engineering and infrastructure from the month building starts. Signups start at 150 a month and grow 15% a month to 1,500. E starts at 225 because content has built an audience by month 10.
  • 40% of signups start using it (45% in E, where builds have taught the builder). 8% of active users stop each month. Each active free user costs $0.60 a month.
  • Agents: 0.25% of active free users start paying each month, at $150 a month. 8% leave each month. Model and messaging costs are 30% of agent revenue.
  • Builds from free users: 0.08% of active free users buy one each month, at $2,500 plus $1,200 a month, because they are smaller firms.
  • In D, doing three things at once cuts signups and agent sales by 20%.
  • Earnings are revenue minus all the costs above and $1,500 a month in general tools. Founder pay beyond build hours is not included.
  • Sale value is 5× a year of earnings outside agents plus 5× a year of agent revenue, both at the month-36 pace, at 10 kr per dollar (rounded).
