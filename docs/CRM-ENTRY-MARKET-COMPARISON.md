# CRM entry-market comparison

**Updated:** 2026-08-31
**Decision:** Preserve both businesses and one shared technical foundation. CRM From Within removes the need for an SME to customize or administer its CRM. Company Native gives an established company an owned replacement it can self-host and change through Company Native, its own team or another partner. Test Company Native’s CRM replacement offer first because the customer value and budget appear larger.

## The two markets

### CRM From Within: the CRM adapts itself for the customer

The customer has no useful CRM. The product removes setup: approved customer work becomes a populated CRM, and expert help is available for valuable additions.

The strongest benefit is relief from CRM design and administration. The company receives a CRM that works quickly, then the system and experts continue adapting it without turning the customer into the product manager for its CRM.

The main alternative is strong and often free. HubSpot Free already includes foundational contact and deal tools for two users. If a generic pipeline is enough, HubSpot or another standard CRM will often be the rational choice.

### Company Native CRM: own and control a deeply adaptable replacement

The customer already has HubSpot, Salesforce or another central CRM. It pays for software, consultants and internal workarounds, but the system still does not fit important sales, delivery or service processes.

The strongest benefit is structural. Company Native can replace recurring licence and customization costs with owned software built exactly around the customer, while handling migration and operational risk. The customer may self-host, choose its AI boundary and keep changing the system through its own team, Company Native or another qualified partner.

The main alternative is staying put. Even an expensive, awkward CRM is known and integrated. The customer will not move merely because custom software sounds better.

## Which creates more customer value?

The migration market appears to create more value for the right customer.

A company without a CRM is comparing CRM From Within with a free or inexpensive standard product. CRM From Within can be much easier and better fitted, but the customer may have weak urgency and little budget.

A company with an established, badly fitted CRM is already paying in several ways:

- licence and add-on fees;
- consultants and administrators;
- internal manual work;
- workarounds in spreadsheets and other systems;
- low adoption and unreliable reporting;
- lost process improvements that the existing product cannot support;
- dependence on a vendor’s pricing, product roadmap and data controls.

When those costs are real and the company uses only a bounded subset of a large platform, an owned replacement can create a substantially larger benefit than a first CRM can create for a small team.

That benefit is conditional. Rebuilding a CRM is probably worse when the customer relies deeply on a vendor’s complete marketing, service, billing, content, mobile and integration ecosystem. Company Native should replace the workflows the customer actually relies on, not promise to rebuild every feature in HubSpot or Salesforce.

## Current market evidence

The addressable installed base is large, but demand to migrate remains unproven.

- HubSpot reported 306,446 customers as of June 30, 2026. Its Sales Hub Professional list price is about $90 per user per month when paid annually, with required onboarding; Enterprise is about $150 per user per month. The full Customer Platform starts far higher. [HubSpot Q2 2026 results](https://ir.hubspot.com/news-releases/news-release-details/hubspot-reports-q2-2026-results), [HubSpot Sales pricing](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales), [HubSpot platform pricing](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/suite)
- Salesforce lists Sales Cloud Enterprise at $175 per user per month and Unlimited at $350, before many add-ons. [Salesforce Sales pricing](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/)
- HubSpot reported that customers associated with solutions partners represented about 25% of customers but about 49% of 2025 revenue. This is evidence that established customers pay heavily for implementation and customization help. It is not evidence that they are willing to replace HubSpot. [HubSpot 2025 annual report](https://ir.hubspot.com/node/15156/html)
- Current AI-native CRMs already populate records from email and meetings. That validates automatic capture but weakens it as a standalone differentiator. [Clarify](https://docs.clarify.ai/en/articles/11702613-what-is-clarify), [Day AI](https://day.ai/crmx)

## Why the same technical core can serve both

Both businesses need reliable infrastructure for identity, permissions, records, email and calendar synchronization, deduplication, provenance, audit, backups, deployment and integrations. Customer workflows and interfaces may still be generated as ordinary code rather than forced into fixed modules.

CRM From Within uses that foundation to generate and operate an adaptive CRM for the customer. Company Native adds system discovery, schema and workflow mapping, historical migration, reconciliation, parallel operation, cutover, code handover and self-hosted deployment.

This means work on a tested CRM core can compound across both businesses without forcing the two customer messages into one site.

## Why they should remain separate offers

The CRM From Within buyer wants to avoid a software project. The Company Native buyer is accepting a major system decision because the current system has become expensive or strategically limiting.

Combining the messages would weaken both:

- “Click and get your CRM” sounds implausible beside enterprise migration and sovereignty claims.
- “We migrate your central system safely” sounds heavy and frightening to a company that only wants to organize its first leads.
- A free or low-cost entry offer creates the wrong price expectation for a high-risk migration.
- Full code ownership and self-hosting create obligations that do not belong in an unproven free product.

The brands can share technology and learning. They should not share one homepage, one primary call to action or one sales process.

## Strategic options considered

### 1. Build CRM From Within first

This can produce more installations and product learning. It also risks attracting companies with low urgency and low willingness to pay before the builder is reliable.

### 2. Build Company Native CRM first — selected for the next live test

This targets a smaller number of customers with visible pain, existing budgets and data that shows what must be preserved. One successful project can fund the reusable CRM core and migration modules. The difficulty is that demand to switch, delivery economics and technical feasibility have not yet been proven.

### 3. Launch both as active businesses immediately

This preserves optionality but splits sales attention, product work and learning before either process is repeatable. The sites and strategies should remain separate, but only one live customer-acquisition and delivery loop should receive serious operating focus at first.

## The selected next test

Find one established HubSpot or Salesforce customer with a costly, poorly fitted workflow. Sell a paid replacement proof with no migration commitment:

1. Map one painful workflow and its current cost.
2. Read the relevant CRM schema, configuration and sample data with permission.
3. Build the owned replacement beside the current system.
4. Let a small group complete the same real work in both systems.
5. Compare usability, work removed, record accuracy, operational risk and credible three-year total cost.
6. Ask the customer to pay for the full migration only after the proof works.

Continue when a customer pays for the proof, prefers the replacement in real work and commits to the next migration step. Change the offer when customers value the prototype but cannot justify migration. Stop the CRM replacement path when serious candidates repeatedly refuse even a bounded paid proof.

## What remains open

- Whether a company will pay for the replacement proof.
- Which HubSpot or Salesforce configurations are bounded enough to replace economically.
- The first credible implementation and maintenance prices.
- Whether three-year total cost can be reduced without shifting risk onto the customer.
- The licence boundary between reusable Company Native modules and customer-owned code.
- How opt-in derived learning works for self-hosted installations without exposing private customer data.
- When CRM From Within should restart active product acquisition after Company Native’s first proof.
- Whether smaller CRM From Within customers will pay for continued adaptation or mainly value the initial setup.
- Whether the shared foundation should use Twenty through its application interfaces, fork its AGPL core or remain independent.
- Whether CRM From Within should use separate deployments for every customer from the beginning or start with isolated workspaces and preserve a later export path.
- Which customer-generated code and outcome evidence may enter the reusable pattern library, under what consent and ownership terms.
